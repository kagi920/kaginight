## Key Question 1 — Platform-based creator income distribution (Patreon, Substack, note.com, Twitch, YouTube), follower→payer conversion, and the effect of memberships/communities

### Takeaway
Every platform with public or leaked data shows an extreme power-law: the top ~1–3% of creators capture roughly 50–85% of money/attention (Patreon top 1% ≥ $2,500/month in 2013–15; Twitch top 1% > 50% of 2021 payouts; YouTube top 3% of channels ≈ 85% of views). Follower→paying conversion is not documented in any peer-reviewed source; the only figures are practitioner estimates of roughly 1–10% (niche publications higher). Memberships are growing fastest in Japan (note.com membership revenue +81.3% YoY as of Nov 2025, secondary report).

### Cited Findings

**Peer-reviewed**

- Regner, T. (2021). "Crowdfunding a monthly income: an analysis of the membership platform Patreon." *Journal of Cultural Economics* 45(1). DOI 10.1007/s10824-020-09381-5. Data = Patreon's first 18 months (2013–15). Top 1% of campaigns (~250 creators) earn ≥ $2,500/month; top 5% (~1,200 creators) earn > $750/month; "the vast majority of campaigns … received zero pledges". Detailed funding cases, images, goal-setting and "thank-you videos" are associated with sustained income; patrons cancel when promises are not kept. — [RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.Org/a/kap/jculte/v45y2021i1d10.1007_s10824-020-09381-5.html); [CultureCase summary](https://culturecase.kdl.kcl.ac.uk/research/2025/09/crowdfunding-regular-income-cultural-projects) [partly verified: numbers from secondary summary]

### Inferences
- With Pareto-tail earnings everywhere, a niche XR drummer should not model income as "followers × platform average"; the realistic planning base is Regner's bottom-95% band (< $750/month) unless a differentiated, high-loyalty niche is built — the ICWSM finding that narrow-pledging patrons are more loyal supports a small, tightly bonded membership.
- The practitioner 1–5% free→paid rate implies that a note.com membership of 50 payers needs ~1,000–5,000 engaged (not merely aware) readers; the 24.8% paid-content share on note shows paid articles are normal there, lowering friction.
- note's membership segment is the fastest-growing monetization line in Japan (+81.3%), so a monthly KAGI ROOM-style membership is better aligned with platform momentum than one-off paid articles.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed estimate of follower→paying-supporter conversion for musicians on Patreon/note/YouTube was found; only blog estimates.
- note's distribution of creator income below the top 1,000 (median, share with zero sales) was not found in public IR.
- Could not open the ICWSM 2022 PDF to extract average pledge, creator/patron counts and churn rates.
- Kickstarter music-category success rates, YouTube Memberships/Super Chat data and Twitch after-2021 distributions were not retrievable (search budget exhausted).

## Key Question 2 — Parasocial interaction / fan identification and willingness to pay or attend (Baym; superfans)

### Takeaway
Relational labor (Baym 2018) is the mechanism; survey studies show parasocial bonds raise purchase intention mostly through attachment/loyalty (Ma et al. 2022, N=288), though one 2025 study found a negative direct link, so the effect is mediated, not automatic. Industry data quantify the payoff: US superfans (≈15% of population) spend $113/month on live music (66% above average) and 73% buy merch vs 26% of general listeners; Spotify "super listeners" are 2% of listeners but 18%+ of streams; MIDiA finds "identity resonance" fans buy merch at ~50% vs ~33% for merely social-media-engaged fans.

### Cited Findings

**Peer-reviewed / academic**

- Baym, N. K. (2018). *Playing to the Crowd: Musicians, Audiences, and the Intimate Work of Connection.* NYU Press, 280 pp., open access. Core concept "relational labor" = continuous interpersonal work of staying connected with fans, distinct from emotional labor; platforms push artist–fan ties toward friendship/family; this work is "personally and financially draining" and labor-intensive. Interviews incl. Billy Bragg, Richie Hawtin, members of the Cure, UB40, Throwing Muses. — [OAPEN open-access](https://library.oapen.org/handle/20.500.12657/89477); [NYU Press Open Square](https://opensquare.nyupress.org/books/9781479815357/); [IJOC review 2020](https://ijoc.org/index.php/ijoc/article/download/15935/3179)
- Ma, et al. (2022). K-pop live-stream audiences, *Frontiers in Psychology* 13: 865698, DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2022.865698. N = 288; parasocial interaction → empathetic attachment and sustained loyalty → purchase intention (both positively associated). — [Frontiers PDF](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.865698/pdf); [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/202ef23368134d8eb0b5212e45c87250) [partly verified: author list not confirmed]
- Krause, A. E., North, A. C., Heritage, B. (2018). "Musician interaction via social networking sites: celebrity attitudes, attachment, and their correlates." *Music & Science* 1. N = 464; celebrity attitudes and attachment style predict how much fans use SNS to interact with musicians. — [JCU repository + preprint](https://researchonline.jcu.edu.au/62740/); [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/8766155fea784ae1b6ced58d1d7239ca)
- Kulczynski, A. (2014). PhD thesis, University of Newcastle (AU): nine motivations drive popular-music concert attendance (hero worship, aesthetics, nostalgia, social interaction, etc.); fan identification measured against attendance frequency. — [NOVA repository](https://nova.newcastle.edu.au/vital/access/ /manager/Repository/uon:14999)
- Chinese audio-knowledge-payment study (2021, City University of Hong Kong record): vocal, social and task attraction strengthen the parasocial bond, which raises willingness to pay (podcast-style audio, not music). — [CityU Scholars](https://scholars.cityu.edu.hk/en/publications/%E4%B8%BA-%E9%99%AA%E4%BC%B4-%E4%B9%B0%E5%8D%95%E5%87%86%E7%A4%BE%E4%BC%9A%E5%85%B3%E7%B3%BB%E8%A7%86%E8%A7%92%E4%B8%8B%E9%9F%B3%E9%A2%91%E7%9F%A5%E8%AF%86%E4%BB%98%E8%B4%B9%E5%86%B3%E7%AD%96%E7%A0%94%E7%A9%B6/)
- Erasmus University MA thesis (2025): fans of Taylor Swift, Harry Styles and two early-career artists; perceived intimacy, reciprocity and authenticity strengthen parasocial relationships, which raise behavioural/purchase intentions. — [thesis.eur.nl](https://thesis.eur.nl/pub/76683/) (student work)
- Tonneau (2025), Ariana Grande fans, N = 235: parasocial relationship had a **negative** direct effect on purchase intention (contradicts Ma et al.). — [Pace Honors thesis](https://digitalcommons.pace.edu/honorscollege_theses/322) (student work)
- Indonesian thesis (2023), N = 170 K-pop fans: parasocial interaction explains ~16% of impulsive merch buying; celebrity worship explains more. — [UIN Sunan Kalijaga](https://digilib.uin-suka.ac.id/cgi/export/eprint/60940/EndNote/digilib-eprint-60940.enw) (student work)
- "Sharing Music: Social and Communal Aspects of Concert-Going" (Networking Knowledge, MeCCSA). — [journal page](https://www.ojs.meccsa.org.uk/index.php/netknow/article/view/428)

**Industry reports**

- Luminate (2023 midyear / 2024 year-end reports): superfans ≈ 15% of US general population (13+, engage with an artist via 5+ of 13 channels); spend 80% more on music per month than average listener (2023 report); 2024: $113/month on live music (+66% vs average; one write-up says +55%), $39/month on physical (+105%); 73% of superfans buy physical merch vs 26% of general listeners; physical buyers are +128% more likely to be superfans; K-pop fans spend 75% more; Latin superfans 30% more than other superfans. — [MBW 15% superfans](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/15-of-the-general-population-of-the-us-are-superfans-heres-what-that-means-for-the-music-business); [MBW Latin superfans](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/latin-music-super-fans-spend-30-more-on-music-related-activities-than-other-music-super-fans-in-the-us-new-luminate-report-find); [Hypebot 2024](https://www.hypebot.com/forget-taylor-swift-these-stats-will-shape-2024-for-99-of-musicians/); [Luminate SXSW piece](https://luminatedata.com/?p=4484)
- Spotify (via MBW): "super listeners" = 2% of an artist's monthly listeners but > 18% of monthly streams. — [MBW](https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/superfans-make-up-2-of-artists-monthly-listeners-on-spotify-but-account-for-over-18-of-monthly-streams)
- MIDiA Research (Q3 2024). "The superfans of music superstars: Identity and cut-through." Survey of 2,009 US consumers on 12 artists' fanbases; "identity resonance" (fandom reflects my identity) is a stronger driver of streams, live and merch than social-media engagement: just under one-third of social-media-engaged fans have bought merch/CD/vinyl vs just over half of identity-resonant fans. — [MIDiA report page](https://midiaresearch.com/reports/the-superfans-of-music-superstars-identity-and-cut-through); [Hypebot summary](https://www.hypebot.com/how-to-grow-an-engaged-fanbase-through-identity-resonance/)
- MIDiA (Q1 2025). "Defining entertainment superfans" — 10,023 respondents across 10 countries incl. Japan; archetypes and annual spend (paywalled). — [MIDiA](https://www.midiaresearch.com/reports/defining-entertainment-superfans-characteristics-categories-and-commercial-impact)
- Soundcharts citing MIDiA/SoundCloud fan-powered-royalties study: fans contributing ≥ $0.10/month = 1.9% of listeners but 42% of revenue (date not given). — [Soundcharts](https://soundcharts.com/blog/what-are-superfans-in-music) **[unverified secondary]**

### Inferences
- The strongest commercial lever is identity, not reach: fans who feel "XR drumming is part of who I am" convert at ~1.5× the merch rate of fans who merely follow — a KAGI NIGHT community should be framed around identity ("KEY holders") rather than content access.
- The negative finding (Tonneau 2025) plus Baym's warning imply over-intimate relational labor can backfire; design interactions that signal reciprocity and authenticity (the three predictors in the Erasmus thesis) rather than volume.
- Superfan spend concentrates in live events and physical goods (not streams), supporting a BASE merch + live-ticket bundle as the primary revenue path.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study directly measuring parasocial strength → concert-ticket purchase for independent (non-star) artists.
- Works by "Pizzo" and "Bonini" named in the brief could not be identified (no hits); if they refer to Bonini & Gandini on podcasts/patronage, nothing was found.
- MIDiA's headline superfan share of music consumers was not visible (paywall).

## Key Question 3 — Personal websites/portfolios and hiring; website aesthetics first impressions; interactive/playful/gamified web experiences and engagement/inquiries

### Takeaway
Rigorous evidence exists for (a) 50 ms aesthetic first impressions that halo onto credibility (Lindgaard 2006; Reinecke 2013/2014, 2.4 million ratings) and (b) interactivity/playfulness → satisfaction, revisit and WOM in consumer web studies (Akrimi 2016; Hsu 2012; Suh 2014; N≈235–292). Gamification raises time-on-task and conversion in reviews (12/15 studies positive; Looyestyn 2017) and one 2026 quasi-experiment (6.9× trial→paid, N≈4,100, app not web). There is **no** peer-reviewed study linking a musician's personal website or 3D/WebGL site to inquiry/booking rates; only vendor case studies (3D embeds: 5–10× time on site; +129% conversion) exist.

### Cited Findings

**Peer-reviewed**

- Lindgaard, G., Fernandes, G., Dudek, C., Brown, J. (2006). "Attention web designers: You have 50 milliseconds to make a good first impression!" *Behaviour & Information Technology* 25(2): 115–126. DOI 10.1080/01449290500330448. Visual-appeal ratings after 50 ms exposure correlate strongly with ratings after long exposure; the affective first impression halos onto later judgments of usability and credibility. — [Nature news 2006](https://www.nature.com/news/2006/060109/full/news060109-13.html); [citation record](https://makinggood.ac.nz/l/lindgaard,-g,-fernandes,-g,-dudek,-c,-brown,-j-(2006)-attention-web-designers-you-have-50-milliseconds-to-make-a-good-first-impression!-behaviour-it,-25,-115–126)
- Reinecke, K., Yeh, T., Miratrix, L., Mardiko, R., Zhao, Y., Liu, J., Gajos, K. Z. (2013). "Predicting users' first impressions of website aesthetics with a quantification of perceived visual complexity and colorfulness." *CHI 2013*. 450 websites, 548 volunteers; colorfulness + visual complexity + age/education explain about half the variance in appeal judged after 500 ms. — [PDF](https://wildlab.cs.washington.edu/Publications_files/Reinecke_CHI2013.pdf); [Harvard page](https://www.eecs.harvard.edu/~kgajos/papers/2013/reinecke13predicting.shtml)
- Reinecke, K., Gajos, K. Z. (2014). "Quantifying Visual Preferences Around the World." *CHI 2014*, pp. 11–20. ~2.4 million ratings from ~40,000 participants; appeal peaks at group-specific colorfulness/complexity; women rate colorful sites higher, higher education weakens that; country differences (e.g., Russia prefers lower complexity). — [Harvard page](https://eecs.harvard.edu/~kgajos/papers/2014/reinecke14visual.shtml); [PDF](https://iis.seas.harvard.edu/papers/reinecke14visual.pdf)

### Inferences
- A WebGL "jellyfish" homepage is consistent with the evidence only if its 50 ms impression is clean (low visual complexity, high prototypicality of "artist site": name, what-I-do, contact above the fold) — playfulness should sit on top of a prototypical layout, not replace it.
- Interactivity evidence predicts higher revisit/WOM, not inquiries per se; the site needs an explicit conversion path (booking/commission form, note/BASE links) because no study shows playfulness alone generating business inquiries.
- Hiring-signal literature (Pallais; Behance practice) implies visible completed work, client names and testimonials matter more than aesthetics for commissions; the homepage should carry a credits/collaborations list.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study on personal websites or SEO for musicians/performers and resulting bookings; no study on 3D/WebGL artist sites. Vendor numbers are the only data and are unverified.

## Key Question 5 — Live-music economics post-2020: hybrid events, streamed-concert revenue, willingness to pay for virtual attendance

### Takeaway
Experimental/ethnographic work (Onderdijk 2021 N=83; Vandenberg 2021 1,501 chat comments; Rendell 2021 three "portal show" cases; Swarbrick 2021) shows livestreams deliver connection to the performer and a sense of community but weaker audience-to-audience solidarity than physical crowds. Surveys show fans will pay for streams but less than in-person: 72% of fans/74% of musicians say livestreams should be paid (Middlesex/KCL 2021, ~1,500 respondents); 29% of UK consumers likely to pay, reasonable price £18.09 (PUSH LIVE 2021); 92.1% refuse in-person-equivalent prices (AMT Lab 2021, n=140); motivation and fanship are the strongest WTP predictors (Hofwegen 2021, n=189). US virtual-concert revenue 2020 ≈ $610m (secondary, unverified).

### Inferences
- For KAGI NIGHT, a hybrid model (physical event + paid stream priced well below the door, ≈ £18/¥3,000 anchor) matches both the WTP data and the finding that performer-connectedness survives mediation while crowd solidarity does not — so the stream should emphasise performer-facing interaction (chat shout-outs, song votes even if agency gains are small) rather than simulate a crowd.
- Fanship predicts stream WTP more than price, so streams convert best when sold to the membership/superfan base, not to cold traffic.
