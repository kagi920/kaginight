## Key Question 1 — Crowdfunding (CAMPFIRE, Makuake, READYFOR, うぶごえ): fees, success rates, case studies

### Takeaway
Japanese reward-crowdfunding fees cluster at 12–20% of funds raised (CAMPFIRE 17%, READYFOR 12%/17%, Makuake 20%, all 税別, with ~5% of that being payment processing); CAMPFIRE's "エンタメ応援プラン" lets entertainment projects set the fee anywhere 0–17%. Music/live-category success-rate and median statistics are **not published**; only platform-wide anecdotal rates (~70–75%) exist. Verified live-show cases on CAMPFIRE in 2024–2025 range from ¥94,050 (small inclusive live-house show) to ¥22.7M (idol one-man + album), with solo-drummer projects landing around ¥1.0–1.3M.

### Cited Findings

**CAMPFIRE fees**
- CAMPFIRE's standard fee is "手数料:17%(税別)" charged only on funds raised; listing is free — [CAMPFIRE industry/highlight page via search](https://camp-fire.jp/highlights/theater) `[snippet]`; also stated in [CAMPFIRE service PDF](https://lp.camp-fire.jp/rs/422-RMC-653/images/CAMPFIRE_service.pdf) `[snippet]`.
- Breakdown: a 2022 campaign notice listed the 17% as "サービス手数料:11%〜12% + 決済手数料:5%" — [value-press / CAMPFIRE press history](https://www.value-press.com/pressrelease/157638) `[snippet]`; the 5% payment-fee component is reused in disaster plans ("サービス手数料を無料に。決済手数料5％のみ") — [campfire.co.jp press 2024-01-09](https://campfire.co.jp/press/2024/01/09/notohantou/) `[snippet]`.
- **CAMPFIRE for Entertainment / エンタメ応援プラン (2023):** project owners in the entertainment category can set the combined 利用手数料+決済手数料 anywhere from **0% to 17% (税別) in 1% steps**, so "プロジェクトオーナーは最大で支援金の全額を受け取ることができます" — [KAI-YOU](https://kai-you.net/article/88375) `[snippet]`.
- CAMPFIRE scale: "12万件以上のプロジェクト…600万人以上の支援者…総額1,200億円を超える支援" — [CAMPFIRE service PDF](https://lp.camp-fire.jp/rs/422-RMC-653/images/CAMPFIRE_service.pdf) `[snippet]`.
- Entertainment category volume: in 2023 "エンターテインメント領域で1600件以上のプロジェクトが立ち上がり、支援総額は23億円以上" — [ASCII.jp](https://ascii.jp/elem/000/004/172/4172692/) `[snippet]`. (Implied average ≈ ¥1.4M per entertainment project — my arithmetic, see Inferences.)
- Platform-wide success rate ~70% is quoted only by an unsourced glossary — [jisaku.com glossary](https://jisaku.com/glossary/crowdfunding) `[unverified]`.

**Makuake fees**
- Makuake 成約手数料: "Makuakeの場合:20%(税抜)" on the 決定代金 when a project succeeds; EC service 20% 税込 — [Makuake 特定商取引法 page](https://www.makuake.com/pages/legal/) `[snippet]`. Payment-processing fee is listed separately but the rate was not visible — [kmong agency listing](https://kmong.com/gig/805451) `[unverified]`.
- Payout timing: from projects ending Feb 2024, funds are paid "プロジェクト終了月の翌月25日" — [paymentnavi](https://paymentnavi.com/?p=142316) `[snippet]`.
- Makuake supports All-or-Nothing and All-in formats — [Makuake help 用語集](https://mkhelp.makuake.com/hc/ja/articles/13755001873049) `[snippet]`.

**READYFOR fees**
- Official notice dated 2024-02-26 announced plan renaming and fee revision effective **2024-04-01**; pre-revision rates were シンプルプラン = 運営手数料7% + 決済手数料5% = 12%, フルサポートプラン = 12% + 5% = 17% (税別) — [corp.readyfor.jp/news/20240226](https://corp.readyfor.jp/news/20240226) `[snippet; post-revision rates not visible in snippet]`.
- A 2026 blog states the current plans are still "シンプルプラン 12%（税別）／フルサポートプラン 17%（税別）" — [deepreview.hatenablog.com](https://deepreview.hatenablog.com/entry/2026/04/20/125730) `[unverified]`; a comparison site says full-support is 22% incl. payment fee — [crexgroup.com](https://crexgroup.com/ja/travel/business/readyfor-fee-reputation-guide/) `[unverified; conflicts]`.
- Historic READYFOR success rate: "掲載プロジェクト8300件の成功率は75％" (2018) — [THE BRIDGE 2018](https://thebridge.jp/2018/07/crowd-funding-readyfor-new-plans) `[snippet; dated]`.

**うぶごえ (Ubugoe) — likely what "uBuy" refers to**
- Creator-side fee is 0%; the service fee is charged to supporters at checkout; All-in and All-or-Nothing both available; snippet also mentions reports of unpaid supporter funds from ~April 2026 — [KAI-YOU word entry](https://kai-you.net/word/%E3%81%86%E3%81%B6%E3%81%94%E3%81%88) `[snippet; the non-payment report is unconfirmed]`.
- No platform named "uBuy" for music crowdfunding was found.

- No 2022–2026 XR-music crowdfunding case with a published final amount was found on Makuake or CAMPFIRE.

### Inferences
- For a self-produced XR drummer, the realistic CAMPFIRE band is the ¥0.8M–¥1.3M range of the solo-drummer cases (100–140 supporters at ~¥9,000 average), not the idol-scale ¥6–22M cases that rely on large existing fanbases.
- Using CAMPFIRE's エンタメ応援プラン at 0% fee (if the project qualifies as entertainment) would materially beat Makuake (20%) and READYFOR (12–17%); but a 0% owner fee presumably shifts cost to supporters or reduces platform promotion — this trade-off was not documented in snippets.
- 2023 entertainment totals (¥2.3B / 1,600 projects ≈ ¥1.4M mean) support ¥1M as a credible single-event goal.

### Gaps
- No official music/live-event category success rate or median raised exists for CAMPFIRE, Makuake or READYFOR (platforms do not publish category stats).
- Post-April-2024 READYFOR official rates not visible; CAMPFIRE official fee page (campfire.jp/fees) could not be opened; Makuake payment-fee % not found.
- "uBuy" could not be identified; うぶごえ assumed.

## Key Question 2 — Merch economics in Japan (BASE, SUZURI, pixivFACTORY, オリジナルプリント.jp, BOOTH; per-head merch spend)

### Takeaway
BASE スタンダード costs 3.6%+¥40 payment fee plus 3% service fee per order (≈6.6%+¥40, no monthly fee); グロースプラン is 2.9% with ¥16,580–19,980/month, break-even around ¥500k monthly sales. SUZURI charges no listing/sales fee: price = 原価 + creator's トリブン, with a ¥176 transfer fee (<¥30k) and payout at end of the month after request. POD T-shirt base costs are ~¥1,350–2,500 (pixivFACTORY) / ~¥1,755 (オリジナルプリント.jp via BASE). The only Japanese survey on live-merch spend (ぴあ総研) shows 37.3% of live-goers buy goods, spending ¥22,386/year on average; no per-show per-head figure exists.

### Cited Findings

**BASE**
- スタンダードプラン: "決済手数料は「注文金額(消費税、送料込み)の3.6%+40円」、サービス利用料は「注文金額の3%」", monthly fee ¥0 — [shopowner-support.net](https://www.shopowner-support.net/?p=168271) `[snippet; cites BASE official]`; same figures in [imitsu.jp](https://imitsu.jp/cost/hp-design/base-commission) `[snippet]`.
- グロースプラン revised 2024-01-16: "決済手数料2.9%＋［年払い］198,960円/年（16,580円/月）または［月払い］19,980円/月"; previously ¥5,980/month — [Impress Watch](https://www.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/news/1387343.html) `[snippet]`. Break-even guidance: "ショップの月商が約50万円を超える場合は…グロースプランの方が安価" — same `[snippet]`.
- BASE on-demand merch (受注生産) partners compared; オリジナルプリント.jp cheapest T-shirt 原価 ¥1,755 — [minikuru EC blog](https://ec.minikuru.co.jp/post-50882/) `[unverified; third-party comparison]`.
- Official BASE fee page https://thebase.com/fee could not be opened `[unverified]`.

**SUZURI (GMOペパボ)**
- Pricing model: selling price = 原価 (production cost) + トリブン (creator margin), set in ¥100 increments; no listing or sales commission; buyer pays shipping — [ecnomikata](https://ecnomikata.com/ecnews/ec_site_operation/26951/) `[snippet]`; [AppBank 2024](https://www.appbank.net/2024/10/28/iphone-application/2747350.php) `[snippet]`.
- Payout: 振込手数料 ¥176 for <¥30,000 (¥275 for ≥¥30,000); transfer from 三井住友銀行; payout "「金にする」を選択した月の翌月末"; auto-request when unpaid トリブン ≥ ¥177 after 5 months — [SUZURI help: トリブンの振込はいつになりますか](https://help.suzuri.jp/hc/ja/articles/115007857247) `[snippet]`; [SUZURI help: トリブンとは](https://help.suzuri.jp/hc/ja/articles/360011006033) `[snippet]`. A 2025-05 blog adds GMOあおぞらネット銀行 transfers are free — [imano-okiniiri](https://imano-okiniiri.com/suzuri-pricing-guide/) `[unverified]`.
- SUZURI T-shirt 原価 figures were **not** found in snippets `[gap]`.

**pixivFACTORY / BOOTH**
- pixivFACTORY standard T-shirt from ¥1,350 (1枚); white front-only/back-only 1枚 ¥1,650, both sides ¥2,500; short-delivery T-shirt ¥1,800 — [pixivFACTORY product pages](https://factory.pixiv.net/products/white_tshirt), [short delivery](https://factory.pixiv.net/products/short_delivery_tshirt) `[snippet]`.
- BOOTH fee: 2020-08 official notice set "5.6%+22円" per order — [BOOTH announcement 452](https://booth.pm/announcements/452) `[snippet]`; from 2025-10-28 raised to 5.6% + ¥45 (home shipping) and warehouse fee changed to price×2.5% + ¥25/item — [note: BOOTH手数料値上げまとめ](https://note.com/imawamada_seiga/n/n3775fbbd742c) `[snippet; secondary]`.

**Per-head merch spending / fan spend surveys**
- ぴあ総研 (2019 survey, 首都圏 18–69): live-goods purchase rate 37.3%; buyers' annual goods spend ¥22,386 per person; 邦楽ロック/ポップス purchase rate 49.8% vs idol 75.4%; annual ticket+goods >¥80,000 — [ぴあ総研 press PDF](https://corporate.pia.jp/news/files/201905%E3%83%A9%E3%82%A4%E3%83%96%E3%82%B0%E3%83%83%E3%82%BA%E3%81%AE%E8%B3%BC%E5%85%A5%E3%81%AB%E9%96%A2%E3%81%99%E3%82%8B%E8%AA%BF%E6%9F%BB%E7%B5%90%E6%9E%9C_%E3%81%B4%E3%81%82%E7%B7%8F%E7%A0%94%E8%AA%BF%E3%81%B9.pdf) `[snippet; 2019, pre-period but the only per-person live-merch survey found]`.
- Favomatch (Dec 2024, n=183 women 18+): 2024 推し活 spend avg ≈¥400,000; 現場関連 ≈¥180,000, グッズ関連 ≈¥170,000; >25% of goods bought from other fans (fleamarket/SNS) — [commercepick](https://www.commercepick.com/?p=59760) `[snippet; small, skewed sample]`. Largest single goods purchase avg ¥44,000 — [commercepick](https://www.commercepick.com/?p=55912) `[snippet]`.
- Oshicoco (Sep 2024): annual goods spend ≈¥30,000–50,000 — [commercepick](https://www.commercepick.com/?p=62870) `[snippet]`.
- afb (Dec 2025, n=500 nationwide): monthly 推し活 spend "~¥5,000" 51.6%; ~70% under ¥10,000/month — [GameBusiness.jp](https://www.gamebusiness.jp/article/2025/12/12/25732.html) `[snippet]`.
- ビデオリサーチ (Jun 2025): majority spend <¥10,000/year; a segment ≥¥500,000 — [Web担](https://webtan.impress.co.jp/n/2025/06/25/49598) `[snippet]`.
- 推し活総研 (Jun 2025, n=11,505): family 推し活 ¥360,000+/person/year, 2.8× solo — [AdverTimes](https://www.advertimes.com/?p=501841) `[snippet]`.
- Live-house F&B spend per head ¥2,000–4,000 (not merch) — [next-business.co.jp](https://next-business.co.jp/dokuritsu-kaigyo/3076/) `[unverified]`.

### Inferences
- Worked example (my arithmetic, using cited fees): a ¥4,000 T-shirt on BASE スタンダード nets ¥4,000 − (3.6%×4,000+40) − 3%×4,000 = ¥3,696 before production; with ¥1,755 POD cost, ≈¥1,940 margin per shirt (~49%). On SUZURI the same ¥4,000 price with (unverified) ¥2,500 原価 yields ¥1,500 トリブン minus a single ¥176 transfer fee per payout.
- Using ぴあ総研's 49.8% rock/pop purchase rate as a ceiling and the afb monthly distribution, a 100-person show plausibly yields 30–50 buyers; applying a ¥3,000–5,000 average ticket gives ¥90k–250k of merch per show — an estimate, not a measured figure.

### Gaps
- No Japanese survey reports merch spend per attendee per show; only annual per-buyer figures (ぴあ総研 2019) exist.
- SUZURI's actual T-shirt 原価 and オリジナルプリント.jp's retail prices were not visible; official BASE/SUZURI/pixivFACTORY fee pages could not be opened.

## Key Question 3 — Brand sponsorship / endorsement for small artists (楽器メーカー programs, micro-sponsorship platforms)

### Takeaway
Japanese instrument endorsements are documented publicly only as "gear provided + listing on the maker's artist page" (Yamaha gave くいだおれ太郎 a CSS-1450A concert snare; Fender supplies instruments to ハマ・オカモト); no source discloses cash amounts, discount rates or contract terms for Pearl/TAMA/Zildjian artists. Zildjian's education-artist page lists artist-discount ordering, product testing and company support but no percentages. Micro-sponsorship matching platforms exist mainly for athletes (スポスタ: free for the athlete side); sponsor-fee benchmarks are only published for eSports teams (¥3–8M/yr mid-tier), not individual musicians.

### Cited Findings
- Yamaha Music Japan signed an endorsement contract with くいだおれ太郎 (Apr 2023); the deal included gifting a concert snare "CSS-1450A" and listing on Yamaha's site/catalog as an artist using Yamaha instruments — [AdverTimes](https://www.advertimes.com/?p=417081) `[snippet]`; [PHILE WEB](https://www.phileweb.com/sp/news/hobby/202304/18/6360.html) `[snippet]`.
- ハマ・オカモト's Fender contract: "この契約に基づいて、フェンダーから楽器の提供を受けることになる" — [livedoor news](https://news.livedoor.com/article/detail/7802174/) `[snippet; 2013, pre-period, illustrative only]`.
- Pearl's Japanese artist page carries interviews and a drummer stating "2015年、僕は正式にPearlと契約しました" — [pearldrum.com/ja/news/artist](https://pearldrum.com/ja/news/artist) `[snippet; terms not disclosed]`.
- Zildjian Education Artist program benefits: direct company support on product decisions, "ordering at an artist discount", participation in product testing, educator community; "very selective about who we allow in"; no % given — [zildjian.com education artist application](https://zildjian.com/pages/education-artist-application) `[snippet]`; endorsement program page linked from [Zildjian FAQ](https://zildjian.com/pages/frequently-asked-questions) `[not opened]`.
- General mechanics (non-JP): manufacturer views endorsement as a commercial relationship where exposure value drives artist value; holding competing brands can end contracts — [stevelawson.net](https://stevelawson.net/?p=1172) `[snippet; opinion piece]`.
- Athlete–sponsor matching services: 2017 launch of a service where athletes and companies post for free and individual fans can give small support — [AMP](https://ampmedia.jp/2017/12/12/athlete-sponcer/) `[snippet]`; スポスタ: matching "原則としてアスリート側とは無料" — [spo-sta.com](https://www.spo-sta.com/sponcer/) `[snippet]`.
- eSports sponsorship benchmarks: top domestic teams ¥10–30M/yr; mid-tier ¥3–8M/yr — [media-radar.jp](https://media-radar.jp/contents/meditsubu/columns6-esports-sponsored/) `[snippet; not music]`.
- Sponsorship can be goods-in-kind as well as cash — [athkatsu.com](https://athkatsu.com/know-how/116/) `[snippet]`.

### Inferences
- For an XR drummer the realistic first-tier is gear/discount-level endorsement (sticks, heads, cymbals, e-drum/trigger makers) plus visibility on a maker's artist page; cash sponsorship at the individual level is undocumented in Japan and should not be budgeted.
- XR/tech-hardware makers (trigger, VR, projection) may be more plausible in-kind sponsors than drum makers, given the "exposure value" logic in endorsement commentary — inference, no Japanese case found.

### Gaps
- No source discloses Pearl/TAMA/Zildjian Japan artist-contract contents (free vs. discount %, exclusivity, duration).
- No Japanese micro-sponsorship marketplace for musicians (SPOTLIGHT, Xtremers) was found; Xtremers pricing not found.
- No "typical amounts" for brand sponsorship of small live events in Japan were found.

## Key Question 4 — Ticketing & streaming (ぴあ / PIA LIVE STREAM, イープラス Streaming+, ZAIKO, Twitch, YouTube): fees, payout, paid-stream conversion

### Takeaway
Organizer-side fees documented: PIA LIVE STREAM 8% sales commission (Lite/Plus plans) plus ¥200 buyer system fee per ticket and a ¥100,000 (税別) management fee on Plus; Streaming+ was 8–15% of ticket revenue at 2020 launch and today bundles ¥198,000/day production separately from a ticket commission; ZAIKO historically 10%+¥50 per ticket (organizer-paid) with an option to pass fees to buyers (0% to organizer) and a current page listing ¥150/stream ticket or ¥50/venue ticket organizer-paid. Twitch subs split 50/50 baseline (70/30 for Partner Plus tiers). No data on paid-stream conversion for small shows exists; consumer surveys show 37.8% of office workers do not want to pay for streams at all.

### Cited Findings
- **PIA LIVE STREAM** (ぴあ brochure ver3.3, Apr 2024): 販売手数料 8% on ticket sales under both Lite (organizer produces) and Plus (PIA produces) plans; Plus has a ¥100,000 (税別) 進行管理費 (ver3.2); organizer bears JASRAC/NexTone costs (PIA advances and offsets); buyer system fee ¥200 (税別) domestic / ¥400 overseas per ticket; コンビニ payment fee ¥200 per transaction — [PIA_LIVE_STREAM_ver3.3.pdf](https://image.pia.jp/uploads5/files/9417/1221/7288/PIA_LIVE_STREAM_ver3.3.pdf), [ver3.2](https://image.pia.jp/uploads5/files/1316/8931/5694/PIA_LIVE_STREAM_ver3.2.pdf) `[snippet; PDF text partly garbled]`. Third-party list gives usual rate 8% and gifting commission ~25% — [ongakusyugi.net](https://www.ongakusyugi.net/899/) `[unverified]`.
- **イープラス Streaming+**: "年間6000件以上の配信"; three types (live / 疑似生 / on-demand), unlimited viewers, max 10 h — [eplus service guide](https://eplus.jp/sf/streamingplus/service-guide) `[snippet]`. Production staffing basic fee "配信管理の基本料金(税込198,000円/1day)" with "視聴チケット販売手数料は別途" — [eplus support page](https://eplus.jp/sf/streamingplus/service-guide/support) `[snippet]`. At 2020 launch, 8–15% of ticket revenue was deducted (payment, system, promotion, server) — [OTOTOY 2020](https://ototoy.jp/feature/2020052209) `[snippet; dated]`.
- **ZAIKO**: 2020 reporting: organizer streaming-ticket fee "通常10%+50円"; from 2020-05-15 organizers can set their fee to 0% by passing it to buyers; subscription (Zaiko Connect) launched — [PR TIMES](https://prtimes.jp/main/html/rd/p/000000012.000051311.html), [digle.tokyo](https://mag.digle.tokyo/news/77324), [ZAIKO press](https://business.zaiko.io/ja/press/press-release-upsell) `[snippet]`. ZAIKO staff interview: "１０００円以上のチケットの場合、主催者側のシステム利用料は1枚につき税抜きで１０％＋５０円（主催者負担の場合）" — [ZAIKO bunka interview 2020](https://business.zaiko.io/ja/press/bunka-interview-2020-oct) `[snippet]`. Current English pricing page: organizer-paid system fee ¥150 per streaming ticket / ¥50 per venue ticket, or buyer-paid, or Custom split — [business.zaiko.io/en/pricing-1](https://business.zaiko.io/en/pricing-1) `[snippet; JP page not seen]`.
- **チケットぴあ buyer fees** (physical tickets): ¥330 per order ≤¥5,000 face / ¥616 >¥5,001, 発券手数料 ¥165/ticket (税込); fee revision Oct 2024 was the first since 2006 — [t.pia.jp charge guide](https://t.pia.jp/guide/charge.jsp) `[snippet; may be pre-revision]`; [J-CAST 2025-08](https://www.j-cast.com/2025/08/29507155.html) `[snippet]`. ## Key Question 5 — Tipping / 投げ銭 (SHOWROOM, 17LIVE, TikTok LIVE, YouTube Super Chat, PayPay/Kyash links)

### Takeaway
Official take-rates are mostly unpublished; secondary sources converge on creator shares of ~30% (SHOWROOM; one site cites an April-2025 official help page), ~30–50% (TikTok LIVE), ~70–75% (17LIVE per a secondary source), 70% (YouTube Super Chat). PayPay/Kyash person-to-person transfers carry no fee (within KYC limits), making direct links the highest-yield tip channel but outside any platform. Japanese data on earnings distribution are thin: 17LIVE reported 8,430 streamers earning ≥¥60,000 in a single month (2020); market-size estimates range ¥50B (NHK) to ¥310B potential (Fintertech 2021).

### Cited Findings
- **SHOWROOM**: third-party guides put the streamer share of gifts at 20–30%; "900ジュエル以上貯まると1ジュエル＝1円で換金" — [streamer-blog.com jewel](https://streamer-blog.com/showroom-jewel/) `[unverified]`; one site says SHOWROOM's official help (updated Apr 2025) lists the gift share at 30.0% — [app-tatsujin](https://app-tatsujin.com/showroom-revenue-structure-2025-2026/) `[unverified; secondary citing official]`. Comparison: "SHOWROOMは還元率の目安が20％～30％なのに対し、YouTube Live/Twitch/ツイキャスは70％が目安" — [kanaderoom](https://www.kanaderoom.jp/mag/nagesen/) `[unverified]`.
- **17LIVE**: gifts 70% / super-tips 75% to streamer per a secondary site citing "17LIVE公式資料2026-01" — [app-tatsujin](https://app-tatsujin.com/?p=66976) `[unverified]`; official streamers get better rates than general ones (no %) — [aumo](https://aumo.jp/articles/2278891) `[unverified]`; payouts via ドットマネー by Ameba since Dec 2021 — [CyberAgent](https://www.cyberagent.co.jp/news/detail/id=26870) `[snippet]`.
- **TikTok LIVE**: conversion rate not published; commonly stated ~30–50% (some reports lower; FX-dependent) — [lifestyle.assist-all.co.jp](https://lifestyle.assist-all.co.jp/?p=30638) `[unverified]`.
- **YouTube Super Chat**: ~30% platform cut / 70% to creator; ¥100 minimum in Japan — see Q4 citations `[unverified secondary]`.
- **PayPay** person-to-person: no fee; methods = QR, phone number, 受け取りリンク (URL via SNS/LINE/mail); limits with eKYC ¥300,000/transfer, ¥1,000,000/30 days (sources conflict on unverified-user limits) — [DIME](https://dime.jp/genre/1907035/), [pay-route](https://pay-route.co.jp/article/?p=6947) `[snippet]`.
- **Kyash**: no user-side transfer fee; revenue from merchant fees — [money-lifehack](https://money-lifehack.com/webservice/fintech/14441) `[snippet; ~2022–24]`.
- **Earnings distribution / market size**: 17LIVE 2020 survey: 8,430 streamers earned ≥¥60,000 in a single month (double the Feb–Apr 2020 figure) — [ITmedia 2022](https://www.itmedia.co.jp/business/spv/2212/25/news023.html) `[snippet]`. 中小企業庁 J-Net21: popular streamers can earn tens of millions of yen from tips, but stable income is hard, so many stream as a side job — [J-Net21 2022-03](https://j-net21.smrj.go.jp/startup/guide/it/20220309.html) `[snippet]`. Market: NHK-reported ¥50B tip market — [PRESIDENT](https://president.jp/articles/-/52032) `[snippet]`; Fintertech 2021 potential ¥310.6B — [livedoor](https://news.livedoor.com/article/detail/21228026/) `[snippet]`.

### Inferences
- Net yield per ¥1,000 tipped: PayPay link ≈¥1,000; YouTube Super Chat ≈¥700; 17LIVE ≈¥700–750 (unverified); TikTok ≈¥300–500; SHOWROOM ≈¥300. A KEY/ONLINE door that routes superfans to a PayPay/Kyash link beats every app on margin but gives up discovery.
- Given 17LIVE's own data (≥¥60k/month was a notable milestone for ~8k streamers nationwide), streaming tips should be modeled as low-four-figure to low-five-figure yen per session for a niche XR performer, absent evidence otherwise.

### Gaps
- No official pages for SHOWROOM/17LIVE/TikTok take-rates were opened; all rates are secondary.
- No Japanese dataset on tip-earnings distribution across streamers (percentiles) was found.

## Key Question 6 — Fan-club / membership platforms (Fanicon, Fantia, pixivFANBOX, Patreon JP, YouTube membership, Discord paid)

### Takeaway
Fee tiers: Fantia 12.5% (non-photo) / 17.5% (photo) from Dec 2025 plus ¥330 per transfer and an 8% buyer-side fee on non-subscription purchases; pixivFANBOX widely reported at 10% + ¥200–300 transfer (official page not opened); Patreon 10% for new creators from Aug 2025 plus payment, 2.5% FX and payout fees; Discord server subscriptions 10% but **unavailable in Japan** (US bank required); Fanicon historically split subscription revenue ~50/50 after app-store 30% (2017), with 2021 ARPU ¥1,272/fan/month (¥562 subscription + ¥710 other) and ~2,500 creators / 212k+ paying fans.

### Cited Findings
- **Fanicon (THECOO)**: launched Dec 2017; creators set monthly fee; Apple/Google 30% deducted on in-app payments, remainder split creator/THECOO; at 2017 launch "月額会費の半分がアイコン側に入る" — [THE BRIDGE 2017](https://thebridge.jp/2017/12/fanicon-release) `[snippet; dated]`. Dec 2021: ~2,213 creator accounts, 161k paying fans, ARPU ¥1,272 (¥562 subscription + ¥710 other); Fanicon FY2021 revenue ¥1.92B; CAGR 130.4% — [Strainer](https://strainer.jp/stories/2740) `[snippet]`. Later: 212k+ fans, 2,500+ creators — [ログミーFinance](https://finance.logmi.jp/articles/376873) `[snippet]`. Musicman interview with CEO 平良真人 on subscription-only core-fan model — [Musicman](https://www.musicman.co.jp/interview/19328) `[snippet]`. Management interview: some band members could quit part-time jobs thanks to Fanicon income — [ログミーFinance](https://finance.logmi.jp/articles/377860) `[snippet; no yen figures]`. Third-party sites list plan prices (¥1,200 / ¥1,500 / ¥3,000–3,500 / ¥6,500) and a 3.5%+¥30 payment fee but conflict with each other — [app-tatsujin](https://app-tatsujin.com/?p=45480) `[unverified; contradictory]`.

### Inferences
- For a music/XR creator (non-adult, non-illustration), Fanicon's model (creator sets ¥500–¥1,500 tiers; ARPU ≈¥1,270 incl. extras) is the closest Japanese analogue to a fan club; but with ~50% share after store fees it is the most expensive per yen, versus FANBOX ~10% or Patreon ~13–15% all-in. Fanicon's value is community tooling and concierge, not margin.
- Discord paid community in Japan must be wired through an external biller (or note membership), which adds a 10–15% layer; this keeps note as a practical back-end even for "non-note" strategies.

### Gaps
- No Fanicon musician case with member count × price × monthly income was found (interviews are qualitative).
- Official fee pages for Fanicon, FANBOX, Fantia (fantia.jp/help), YouTube membership were not opened; Fanicon's current creator share is unknown (2017's 50% may be outdated).
- No survey on Japanese fans' annual membership spend per artist specifically (only aggregate 推し活 spend in Q2).
