# XR / VR / AR Live Music Performance and Audience Participation — Academic Literature (2015–2026)

Research notes for positioning Kyoichi Kagiyama ("XR Drummer", KAGI NIGHT, Tokyo) as research-grounded. Compiled 2026-10-14.

**Verification status legend**
- ✔ = existence, authors, venue confirmed from a search result pointing at the publisher/repository/proceedings page.
- (DOI unverified) = paper confirmed to exist, but the DOI string is from memory or a secondary citation and could not be resolved (doi.org was blocked by the proxy during this session). Treat DOI as "check before citing"; the stable URL given is the safe handle.
- UNVERIFIED = I could not confirm the paper from any retrieved source in this session. Do not cite without checking.

**Method note:** ~27 web searches were run (ACM/IEEE/NIME/Frontiers/repository hits). Full-text fetches failed (network), so findings come from abstracts, repository records and search snippets. Where a number is quoted it appeared verbatim in the abstract/record.

## KQ1. Canonical references (full citations, grouped by theme)

### Takeaway
A defensible core canon of ~25 papers exists, concentrated in Frontiers in Psychology (livestream/VR concert psychology, 2017–2023), NIME/SMC/IEEE MultiMedia (audience participation, 2012–2024), ISMAR/IEEE VR/Frontiers in VR (VR concert avatars & volumetric performers, 2020–2025), ACM CHI/CSCW/DIS (remote and social-VR music audiences, 2021–2026), and IEEE Access/Personal & Ubiquitous Computing (networked performance and the "Musical Metaverse"). Open-access share is high (Frontiers, NIME, MDPI, arXiv, institutional repositories).

### Cited Findings

#### A. Presence, liveness and social connection in streamed / VR concerts

- **Onderdijk, K. E., Swarbrick, D., Van Kerrebroeck, B., Mantei, M., Vuoskoski, J. K., Maes, P.-J., & Leman, M. (2021). "Livestream Experiments: The Role of COVID-19, Agency, Presence, and Social Context in Facilitating Social Connectedness." *Frontiers in Psychology*, 12:647929. DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2021.647929. ✔ Open access.** Three livestreamed concerts, 83 complete response sets. Physical presence predicted connectedness with both artist and audience; social presence predicted connectedness with audience only; VR-headset viewers reported more physical presence and more artist connection than YouTube viewers; Zoom raised social presence but lowered artist connection vs. YouTube; letting viewers vote on the final song did not increase agency, but hearing a preferred song raised artist connectedness. — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.647929/full); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8180877); [RITMO PDF](https://uio.no/ritmo/english/projects/entrainment-social-bonding-pleasure/quarantine-concerts/publications/livestream-experiments.pdf)
- **Swarbrick, D., Seibt, B., Grinspun, N., & Vuoskoski, J. K. (2021). "Corona Concerts: The Effect of Virtual Concert Characteristics on Social Connection and Kama Muta." *Frontiers in Psychology*, 12:648448 (DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2021.648448 — DOI unverified). ✔ Open access.** Survey of 307 participants from 13 countries on how virtual-concert features (live vs. pre-recorded, platform, chat, etc.) relate to social connection and being moved (kama muta). — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8260031); referenced in the Livestream Experiments search summary.
- **Swarbrick, D., Bosnyak, D., Livingstone, S. R., Bansal, J., Marsh-Rollo, S., Woolhouse, M. H., & Trainor, L. J. (2019). "How Live Music Moves Us: Head Movement Differences in Audiences to Live Versus Recorded Music." *Frontiers in Psychology*, 9:2682. DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2018.02682. ✔ Open access.** Motion capture at a live album-release concert vs. playback of the same album: head movements were faster in the live condition; fans moved faster and showed more rhythmic entrainment than neutral listeners — direct empirical support for the value of liveness. — [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6336707/); [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.02682/full)
- **Charron, J.-P. (2017). "Music Audiences 3.0: Concert-Goers' Psychological Motivations at the Dawn of Virtual Reality." *Frontiers in Psychology*, 8:800. DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00800. ✔ Open access (mini-review).** Frames live concerts as the industry's growing revenue core; argues mediated performances typically lack presence/authenticity but that spherical video and VR goggles "could represent a new form of experiencing live music". Widely cited as the early marketing-side statement of the VR-concert opportunity. — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00800/full); [Altmetric](https://altmetric.com/details/20432526)
- **Onderdijk, K. E., Bouckaert, L., Van Dyck, E., & Maes, P.-J. (2023). "Concert experiences in virtual reality environments." *Virtual Reality* (Springer). (DOI 10.1007/s10055-023-00814-y — DOI unverified.) ✔ Open access via PMC.** Survey of 74 VR-concert attendees: positive evaluations and future-use intentions were significantly influenced by experienced immersivity. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10239717/); [Tilburg record](https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/concert-experiences-in-virtual-reality-environments/)
- **Tschacher, W., et al. (2023). "Audience synchronies in live concerts illustrate the embodiment of music experience." *Scientific Reports* (Oct 2023). ✔ Open access.** 132 audience members across three public classical concerts: significant synchrony in heart rate, skin conductance and respiration rate (not breathing phase); synchrony — especially heart-rate synchrony — was higher when listeners felt moved, inspired and immersed. A follow-up extended to ~700 attendees over 11 concerts. — [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10556000/); [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/22947de171a8455e851f349b63c59c8a)
- **Leeds-linked within-subjects study (authors not captured): 360° VR classical concert vs. desktop video with headphones.** VR significantly increased pleasantness and all measured presence dimensions; emotional arousal did not differ. — [White Rose eprint 172355](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/172355/) (authors/venue need checking)
- **Project MultiLive (University of Southampton), "Empirical Insights into Concert Streaming: Liveness and Audience Experience."** Compared in-person attendance with collective live-stream viewing of a chamber concert; liveness depends on both immediate sensory perception and conscious reflection (alienation vs. empathy). — [screeningliveperformance.soton.ac.uk](https://screeningliveperformance.soton.ac.uk/?p=437) (project page; locate the journal article before citing)

## KQ2. Quantitative findings on engagement, presence, willingness to pay, retention

### Takeaway
Quantitative evidence is modest in scale (n≈7–307 per study) but consistent: VR/HMD viewing raises presence and artist connection; audience-avatar liveliness raises presence; liveness increases bodily and physiological engagement; WTP for fully simulated VR concerts clusters around €35–39.99 and is driven by attitude, perceived ease of use/usefulness and income. No peer-reviewed retention (repeat-attendance) data were found.

### Cited Findings
- 83 livestream respondents: VR headset > YouTube on physical presence and artist connection; Zoom > YouTube on social presence but < on artist connection; song-vote did not raise agency; loneliness predicted artist connection. — [Onderdijk et al. 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.647929/full)
- 307 participants, 13 countries surveyed on virtual-concert characteristics vs. social connection and kama muta. — [Swarbrick et al. 2021, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8260031)
- 74 VR-concert attendees: evaluations and future perspectives significantly influenced by experienced immersivity. — [Onderdijk et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10239717/)
- Head movement faster at live concert than recorded playback; fans more entrained than neutral listeners. — [Swarbrick et al. 2019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6336707/)
- 132 concertgoers: significant synchrony in heart rate, skin conductance, respiration rate; higher when moved/immersed; openness and agreeableness ↑ synchrony, neuroticism and extraversion ↓. — [Tschacher et al. 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10556000/); [PsyPost summary](https://www.psypost.org/harmony-in-heartbeats-berlin-concert-study-reveals-synchrony-in-classical-music-listeners/)
- Presence (IPQ) increased with proportion of user-controlled audience avatars (0/50/100%), n=7 pilot. — [Guang et al. 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/virtual-reality/articles/10.3389/frvir.2025.1655545/abstract)
- Chat-driven artificial crowd reactions: n=48, stronger immersion and co-presence than prior NPC-audience method. — [arXiv 2503.13121](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13121)
- 360° VR vs desktop classical concert: VR significantly higher pleasantness and all presence dimensions; arousal unchanged. — [White Rose 172355](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/172355/)
- Remote audience rated a streamed theatre production less intense and less cohesive than the co-present audience; distributed two-venue production integrated audiences better. — [Geelhoed et al. 2016](https://www.joanneum.at/digital/publikationen/detail/co-present-and-remote-audience-experiences-intensity-and-cohesion)
- WTP for fully simulated VR concerts: purchase intention driven by payment attitude > perceived behavioural control > subjective norms; WTP shaped by monthly income, attitude and purchase intention; acceptable price grows with perceived usefulness/ease of use; most common WTP band €35–39.99. — [Bär et al. 2025](https://www.elsevier.es/en-revista-journal-innovation-knowledge-376-articulo-on-edge-digital-event-era-S2444569X25001210)
- 2021 Nova SBE MSc thesis: virtual concertgoers more satisfied but willing to pay less than for in-person. — [NOVA repository](https://run.unl.pt/handle/10362/123402?locale=en) (student thesis)
- Spatial audio raised AV-sync tolerance to ~461 ms vs ~224 ms stereo (n=18, student thesis). — [LUP](https://lup.lub.lu.se/student-papers/record/9233095/file/9233107.pdf)
- Hearing-impaired vs normal-hearing immersion did not differ significantly in an Ambisonic concert (n=23). — [SMC 2024](https://smcnetwork.org/smc2024/papers/SMC2024_paper_id75.pdf)
- Networked performance: delay >~20–25 ms one-way progressively slows tempo; <11.5 ms slight speed-up. — [Stanford MediaX](https://mediax.stanford.edu/research-projects/lat-chafe); review in [Rottondi et al. 2016](https://doaj.org/article/b6289c0973fd4f6d9543aaef43acdb70)
- Social-VR music scene: 84 survey / 27 interviews / 17 observations; free-access expectation vs. unpaid labour tension. — [Ppali et al. CHI 2026](https://iris.unitn.it/retrieve/191a8c8c-e138-4d65-b07c-c87f73bd6e8a/3772318.3791497.pdf)

### Inferences
- A hybrid show can cite Onderdijk 2021 to justify (a) offering an HMD/VR tier for artist connection and (b) a chat/co-watch layer for audience connection — the two presence types map to KAGI NIGHT's venue + metaverse (Virtual B.Y.G.) split.
- Bär 2025's €35–39.99 band is a usable benchmark against KAGI NIGHT's ¥4,100 + 1 drink real-venue ticket and free metaverse admission with in-app support items (see KQ4).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed retention/churn or repeat-attendance statistics for XR or hybrid concerts were found.
- No Japanese-market WTP study surfaced.
- Effect sizes for Swarbrick 2021 and Onderdijk 2023 not captured (full text not fetched).

## KQ3. Validated design principles for audience participation in live music

### Takeaway
Across NIME/SMC/IEEE MultiMedia and the Frontiers livestream work, the recurring validated principles are: zero-install, phone-native participation; constrained musical agency that preserves coherence; clear audience-to-outcome feedback (perceived control); low latency; balancing musician, audience and musical-coherence needs; and designing separately for physical presence (with artist) and social presence (with crowd).

### Cited Findings
- Constrained phone instrument (harmony-locked): easy to use; participants felt connected to music and other musicians. — [echobo, NIME 2013](https://nime.org/proc/nime2013_lee_a/)
- Zero-install via captive portal/web lowers barrier for mass participation. — [SWARMED, NIME 2013](https://nime.org/proc/nime2013_hindle/); [massMobile, NIME 2012](https://nime.org/proc/nime2012_weitzner/)
- Voting on musical attributes: positive engagement/creativity, but audiences worried about sense of control and latency → feedback and responsiveness are design requirements. — [Open Symphony, NIME 2016](https://nime.org/proc/nime2016_zhang/)
- TMAP design issues: expressiveness, communication, appropriateness; musicians, audiences and musical coherence have different requirements; TMAP Design Cards as a tool. — [Hödl et al. 2017](https://oro.open.ac.uk/52731); [Hödl 2016 thesis](https://repositum.tuwien.at/handle/20.500.12708/6615); [TMAP cards chapter](https://eprints.cs.univie.ac.at/6284/1/Pages%20from%202019_Book_NewDirectionsInMusicAndHuman-C.pdf)
- Phones as composed instruments in chamber music: engaging for audience and stage musicians alike. — [Egozy & Lee, NIME 2018](https://nime.org/proc/nime2018_egozy/)
- Phones as light/movement sources with computer vision — participation without requiring sonic input. — [Pharosphones, NIME 2024](https://nime.org/proc/nime2024_70/)
- Voting alone did not create felt agency; design for physical and social presence instead. — [Onderdijk et al. 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.647929/full)
- Audience-avatar liveliness (user-controlled or synthesized motion) raises presence in sparse VR crowds. — [Yakura & Goto 2020](https://staff.aist.go.jp/m.goto/PAPER/ISMAR2020yakura.pdf); [Guang et al. 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/virtual-reality/articles/10.3389/frvir.2025.1655545/abstract); [arXiv 2503.13121](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13121)
- In-person and remote audiences want different things; hybrid design should give each a distinct offer plus cross-contact. — [StoryFutures 2023](https://pure.royalholloway.ac.uk/en/publications/experiments-in-real-time-hybrid-performance-innovating-in-the-cap)
- Remote audiences need platform-specific engagement affordances; unmet needs documented. — [Ppali et al. 2025](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/509258)
- Multi-user immersive music environments lack standard networking/sync stacks — plan for bespoke infrastructure. — [Boem et al., NIME 2024](https://nime.org/proc/nime2024_33/)

### Inferences
- For a drummer-led show, tempo-locked/beat-synchronized participation (tapping, light) is the principle most directly supported (echobo, Yakura & Goto's beat-synced avatars, Pharosphones).

### Gaps
- No systematic review/meta-analysis of technology-mediated audience participation post-2020 was found this session.
- Effect of latency thresholds specifically on *audience* (not performer) participation apps is not quantified in the retrieved sources.

## KQ4. Japan / Asian-market coverage (VR concerts, virtual idols, volumetric streaming, Beat Wave)

### Takeaway
Japanese academic coverage is strongest on VR-concert audience avatars (AIST/Tsukuba 2020; UEC/Ritsumeikan 2025), VTuber/virtual-idol reception (CHI 2021 Yokohama; Vocaloid liveness), Keio KMD's Tokyo–Sydney volumetric live, and an arXiv study of idol culture under COVID. Beat Wave appears only in trade press (MoguraVR) — as the volumetric streaming app used for Kagiyama's own KAGI NIGHT 2022 — not in peer-reviewed literature.

### Cited Findings

- Guang, Sakurai, Matsumura, Okafuji (UEC/Ritsumeikan), Frontiers in VR 2025: presence rises with share of user-controlled audience avatars. — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/virtual-reality/articles/10.3389/frvir.2025.1655545/abstract)
