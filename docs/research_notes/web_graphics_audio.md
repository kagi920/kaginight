# Web graphics & audio literature behind the kaginight WebGL1 point-cloud jellyfish site

Scope: technical / academic references for the engineering choices of https://kagi920.github.io/kaginight/ (raw WebGL1 point-cloud jellyfish, ported from holtsetio/aurelia; procedural Web Audio D-dorian loop synced to bell contraction; SOUND button only, no autoplay; `GL_FRAGMENT_PRECISION_HIGH` fallback on Android). Research date: 2026-10-14.

Access limits during this research: dl.acm.org, api.crossref.org and scitepress.org were unreachable from the sandbox, so some DOIs are taken from secondary records and are marked **unverified**. Everything marked "OA" has a freely downloadable copy at the link given.

## Key Question 1: Procedural jellyfish / medusa animation & simulation (graphics + the biology it cites)

### Takeaway
The canonical graphics paper is Rudolf & Mould (2009), which simulates a jellyfish as a spring-mass bell coupled to a 2D semi-Lagrangian fluid via the immersed boundary method and exploits axial symmetry; the biology it draws on is the Dabiri/Costello/Colin/Gemmell line of work on vortex-ring propulsion and "passive energy recapture". The direct ancestor of the site's aesthetic in the web world is Rodic's *Chrysaora* (SIGGRAPH 2011 Real-Time Live!) and holtsetio/aurelia (2025, three.js WebGPU).

### Cited Findings
- **Rudolf, D. & Mould, D. (2009). "Interactive Jellyfish Animation Using Simulation." Proc. GRAPP/VISIGRAPP 2009 (SciTePress).** Simulates the elastic bell with a spring-mass model and the surrounding water with a grid-based semi-Lagrangian solver, coupled by a modified immersed boundary method; axially symmetric species are simulated as a low-resolution 2D slice and extrapolated to 3D, with noise added to the bell "inspired by empirical observations of real jellyfish"; an open-loop controller drives muscle contraction. OA PDF (Carleton GIGL mirror): https://gigl.scs.carleton.ca/papers/jelly.pdf ; publisher record https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2009/17924 (DOI not retrievable from sandbox — **unverified**). — [SciTePress](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2009/17924); [Carleton PDF](https://gigl.scs.carleton.ca/papers/jelly.pdf)
- **Rudolf, D. (2007). "Animating jellyfish through numerical simulation and symmetry exploitation." MSc thesis, University of Saskatchewan.** Same method in thesis form; OA at the university repository. — [HARVEST / USask](https://harvest.usask.ca/items/ffd0cee1-8aea-46c2-b785-454298be2fb7/full)
- **rand-monster/jellyfish (GitHub).** Describes itself as a true-3D, WebGPU-compute-shader version of Rudolf & Mould's formulation with no keyframed animation — evidence that the 2009 paper is still the reference model for real-time browser jellyfish. — [GitHub](https://github.com/rand-monster/jellyfish)
- **Rodic, A. (2011). "Chrysaora: WebGL Jellyfish Simulation." SIGGRAPH 2011 Real-Time Live!, Vancouver (Savannah College of Art and Design).** Real-time jellyfish built with JavaScript, WebGL, WebSockets and CSS; multiple browsers were synchronized via WebSocket; it began as a commission for a SCAD Hong Kong gallery video installation and was built as a real-time simulation instead of a video. Still runnable at Google Chrome Experiments. — [ACM SIGGRAPH History Archives](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=73397); [SIGGRAPH 2011 Real-Time Live!](https://history.siggraph.org/experience-overview/siggraph-2011-real-time-live/); [Chrome Experiments](https://experiments.withgoogle.com/chrysaora)
- **holtsetio/aurelia (GitHub, 2025).** Realtime procedural jellyfish renderer built on three.js `WebGPURenderer`; last push 2025-06-10, ~151 stars / 21 forks per a third-party tracker (GitHub itself unreachable from sandbox; figures may be stale). The kaginight site is a WebGL1 port of this. — [gittrend.io mirror](https://gittrend.io/repo/holtsetio/aurelia)
- **Dabiri, J.O., Colin, S.P., Costello, J.H. & Gharib, M. (2005). "Flow patterns generated by oblate medusan jellyfish: field measurements and laboratory analyses." J. Exp. Biol. 208: 1257–1269.** Starting and stopping vortices merge into a lateral vortex superstructure that serves both feeding and propulsion. DOI 10.1242/jeb.01519 — **unverified** (not returned by search). — [RWU faculty record](https://docs.rwu.edu/fcas_fp/110)
- **Dabiri, J.O., Colin, S.P., Katija, K. & Costello, J.H. (2010). "A wake-based correlate of swimming performance and foraging behavior in seven co-occurring jellyfish species." J. Exp. Biol. 213: 1217–1225.** Ties behaviour to a threshold between an isolated vortex ring and a ring with a trailing jet. OA PDF via WHOI. — [WHOI PDF](https://www.whoi.edu/science/AOPE/people/kyoung/Dabiri_etal_JExpBiol_2010.pdf)
- **Gemmell, B.J., Costello, J.H., Colin, S.P., Stewart, C.J., Dabiri, J.O., Tafti, D. & Priya, S. (2013). "Passive energy recapture in jellyfish contributes to propulsive advantage over other metazoans." PNAS 110(44): 17904–17909. DOI 10.1073/pnas.1306983110.** Jellyfish travel ~30% further per swimming cycle via passive energy recapture in the relaxation phase — the biological justification for a contraction/relaxation cycle driving a visual pulse. OA via Caltech Authors. — [Caltech Authors](https://resolver.caltech.edu/CaltechAUTHORS:20190422-155747277); [MBL press](https://www.mbl.edu/news/built-efficiency-not-speed-scientists-discover-new-propulsive-mechanism-jellyfish)
- **Gemmell, B.J., Costello, J.H. & Colin, S.P. (2014). "Exploring vortex enhancement and manipulation mechanisms in jellyfish that contributes to energetically efficient propulsion." Commun. Integr. Biol. 7(4).** A second "stopping vortex" of opposite spin contributes to high propulsive efficiency. OA via PMC. — [PMC4203578](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4203578)
- **Rasmussen et al. (2009). arXiv:0910.3242.** Computational simulation of vortex dynamics from recorded jellyfish strokes using Dabiri's data; OA preprint. — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0910.3242)
- **Crumley, J. et al. (2012), University of Cape Town.** A later jellyfish-animation paper hosted by UCT (cited among Rudolf-related work; details not verified). — [UCT PDF](https://people.cs.uct.ac.za/~Jgain/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/crumley2012.pdf)

### Inferences
- A talk can honestly describe the kaginight jellyfish as a *procedural, non-keyframed* bell driven by a contraction phase, in the lineage Rudolf & Mould (2009) → Chrysaora (2011) → aurelia (2025) → kaginight (WebGL1 port), with the contraction/relaxation cycle referencing Gemmell et al. 2013 — provided the site's actual code is a phase-driven deformation rather than a fluid simulation (state this precisely; do not claim fluid simulation unless true).

### Gaps
- Rudolf & Mould DOI and page numbers not retrievable (SciTePress blocked). Dabiri 2005 DOI unverified.
- Costello & Colin 1994/1995 morphology papers were not returned by search; omitted rather than guessed.
- No SIGGRAPH/Eurographics *soft-body jellyfish* paper later than 2012 was found in this pass; general soft-body (position-based dynamics, Müller et al. 2007) was out of scope for the search budget.

## Key Question 2: Point-based rendering, particle systems, GPU particles, mobile WebGL performance and shader precision

### Takeaway
Reeves (1983) founded particle rendering, Zwicker et al. (2001) founded modern point splatting, Kipfer et al. (2004) moved particle simulation onto the GPU, and Schütz & Wimmer (2019/2021) are the current reference for high-throughput point rendering. For mobile, the key *normative* fact is the GLSL ES 1.00 spec: `highp` in fragment shaders is optional and must be guarded with `GL_FRAGMENT_PRECISION_HIGH` — exactly the fallback the kaginight engine implements.

### Cited Findings
- **Reeves, W.T. (1983). "Particle Systems—A Technique for Modeling a Class of Fuzzy Objects." ACM Transactions on Graphics 2(2): 91–108. DOI 10.1145/357318.357320.** Introduces modelling of fire/clouds/water as clouds of stochastically generated primitive particles; developed at Lucasfilm for the Genesis sequence of *Star Trek II*. OA mirror at Princeton. — [Princeton PDF](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/spr15/cos426/papers/Reeves83.pdf); [SIGGRAPH archive](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=106060); [Wikipedia (DOI)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_system)
- **Zwicker, M., Pfister, H., van Baar, J. & Gross, M. (2001). "Surface Splatting." Proc. SIGGRAPH 2001, pp. 371–378.** Screen-space EWA filtering to render opaque/transparent surfaces directly from point clouds without connectivity. DOI commonly given as 10.1145/383259.383300 — **unverified** (ACM DL unreachable). OA via Harvard VCG and MERL TR2001-20. — [Harvard VCG](https://vcg.seas.harvard.edu/publications/20010101-surface-splatting); [MERL TR](https://shadow.merl.com/publications/docs/TR2001-20.pdf); [SIGGRAPH archive](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=116931)
- **Kipfer, P., Segal, M. & Westermann, R. (2004). "UberFlow: A GPU-Based Particle Engine." Graphics Hardware 2004, pp. 115–122. DOI 10.2312/EGGH/EGGH04/115-122.** Keeps all particle data on the GPU because bus bandwidth was the bottleneck; GPU sorting network for visibility, inter-particle collisions. OA at TUM and Eurographics DL. — [TUM PDF](https://www.cs.cit.tum.de/fileadmin/w00cfj/cg/Research/Publications/2004/GPU_Particle_Engine/eghw04.pdf); [EG DL](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/EGGH.EGGH04.115-122)
- **Schütz, M. & Wimmer, M. (2019). "Rendering Point Clouds with Compute Shaders." SIGGRAPH Asia 2019 Posters. arXiv:1908.02681.** Compute-shader rasteriser up to 10× faster than `GL_POINTS`, 40-bit depth, splat blending. OA. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.02681); [TU Wien](https://www.cg.tuwien.ac.at/research/publications/2019/SCHUETZ-2019-PCC/)
- **Schütz, M., Kerbl, B. & Wimmer, M. (2021). "Rendering Point Clouds with Compute Shaders and Vertex Order Optimization." Computer Graphics Forum 40(4) (EGSR 2021). DOI 10.1111/cgf.14345.** Up to an order of magnitude over the hardware pipeline; 796 M points at 62–64 fps on RTX 3090 without LOD. OA (arXiv 2104.07526 / TU Wien). — [EG DL](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf14345); [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2104.07526)
- **Khronos, OpenGL ES Shading Language 1.00 (rev. 17), §4.5 Precision and Precision Qualifiers.** Quoted by multiple implementers: `GL_FRAGMENT_PRECISION_HIGH` is defined to 1 when the fragment language supports `highp` and is undefined otherwise; §4.5.2: "The fragment language requires any uses of lowp and mediump to compile without error. Support for highp is optional."; §4.5.3: the fragment language has no default float precision, so a precision qualifier or `precision` statement is mandatory. — [NVIDIA-hosted spec PDF](https://docs.nvidia.com/drive/active/5.0.10.3L/nvvib_docs/NVIDIA%20DRIVE%20Linux%20SDK%20Development%20Guide/baggage/GLSL_ES_Specification_1.0.17.pdf); [Linaro patch quoting §4.5.2](https://patches.linaro.org/patch/15444); [Mesa bug quoting §4.5](https://bugs.freedesktop.org/show_bug.cgi?id=31673); [wlroots MR on highp ifdef](https://gitlab.freedesktop.org/wlroots/wlroots/-/merge_requests/3997)
- **Imagination Technologies, `GL_OES_fragment_precision_high` extension doc.** Vendor-side description of the same macro. — [docs.imgtec.com](https://docs.imgtec.com/reference-manuals/open-gl-es-extensions/html/topics/GL_OES/fragment-precision-high.html)
- **Toasa et al. (2019). "Performance Evaluation of WebGL and WebVR Apps in VR Environments." ISVC 2019 (Springer).** Unity stress test with many objects and particle systems across devices/browsers. — [UPS record](https://pure.ups.edu.ec/en/publications/performance-evaluation-of-webgl-and-webvr-apps-in-vr-environments/)
- **Florez et al. (2021). "Performance of WebGL standard for displaying 3D applications on mobile devices." CISTI 2021 (IEEE).** Compression formats, connection speed and browser choice all affect load time, performance and build size on mobile; UX "can be acceptable or deplorable". Full text restricted. — [UNIR repository](https://reunir.unir.net/items/245800a5-2e76-4a16-a87f-06219647fa7f/full)
- **Goukouni et al. (KFUPM). "Methods tested to optimize the performance of WebGL applications."** Tests techniques to lower GPU memory footprint, speed initialisation and improve rendering across browsers, desktop and mobile. — [KFUPM Pure](https://pure.kfupm.edu.sa/en/publications/methods-tested-to-optimize-the-performance-of-webgl-applications/)
- **Basemark Web 3.0 / Browsermark 2.0 (industry benchmarks).** Browser benchmarks with WebGL tests; Basemark Web 3.0 adds battery/power measurement under web workloads. — [Khronos news](https://www.khronos.org/news/permalink/rightware-introduces-browsermark-2.0-with-tests-for-webgl-css3-html5-and-mo); [GPUScore](https://www.gpuscore.com/?p=65)
- **Informal developer data (non-peer-reviewed):** max WebGL canvas size differs by browser (Safari 4096², Chromium 5760²). — [nano-pow benchmarks](https://unpkg.com/nano-pow@5.2.2/docs/benchmarks.md)

### Inferences
- The kaginight precision fallback (`#ifdef GL_FRAGMENT_PRECISION_HIGH`, pass time as `t % period` under mediump) is a direct implementation of GLSL ES 1.00 §4.5; this spec section is the correct citation, not a paper.
- Rendering with `GL_POINTS` on WebGL1 is the "classic point-based rendering" baseline that Schütz & Wimmer measure against; citing them positions the site's choice as deliberate simplicity for compatibility rather than peak throughput.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study specifically quantifying mediump/highp artefacts on Android GPUs was found; evidence is spec text plus implementer bug reports.
- Latta (GDC 2004) "Building a Million Particle System" and Kolb/Latta/Rezk-Salama (2004) were not returned by search; not cited.
- Zwicker 2001 DOI unverified.

## Key Question 3: Bloom / HDR tone mapping / cheap blur on WebGL1 (Kawase, dual filter, Jimenez 2014, Hable, ACES, Reinhard)

### Takeaway
The bloom/blur toolkit the site's aesthetic belongs to is: Kawase's GDC 2003 multi-pass blur (pseudo-HDR on LDR buffers), Bjørge's SIGGRAPH 2015 "dual filter" for mobile bandwidth, and Jimenez's SIGGRAPH 2014 pyramidal bloom; tone-mapping choices are documented by Reinhard 2002, Hable GDC 2010 (filmic A–F curve) and Narkowicz's 2016 ACES fit. A 2025 Android benchmark confirms mipmap/Kawase pipelines are the cheapest on mobile.

### Cited Findings
- **Kawase, M. (2003). "Frame Buffer Postprocessing Effects in DOUBLE-S.T.E.A.L (Wreckless)." GDC 2003.** Multi-pass blur where each pass blends four 2×2 blocks with growing offsets; originally made for bloom; approximates Gaussian cheaply. Original slides not located in this pass (secondary sources only). — [Intel developer page](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/developer/videos/improving-real-time-gpu-based-image-blur-algorithms-kawase-blur-and-moving-box-averages.html); [Mynavi (Japanese) on DOUBLE-S.T.E.A.L pseudo-HDR](https://news.mynavi.jp/article/graphics-50/); [kawase_halide write-up](https://github.com/geoffoxholm/kawase_halide)
- **Bjørge, M. (2015). "Bandwidth-Efficient Rendering." SIGGRAPH 2015 course *Moving Mobile Graphics* (Arm).** Source of the "dual filter" (dual Kawase) down/up-sample blur for mobile bloom; cited as prior art in EP 4248396 A4. Arm-hosted notes PDF could not be opened from the sandbox (**unverified** content). — [SIGGRAPH history profile](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=59817); [EPO patent citing it](https://data.epo.org/gpi/EP4248396A4)
- **Arm Mobile Graphics blog, "Post-processing effects on mobile: optimization and alternatives."** Documents bandwidth cost of a full RGB24 bloom buffer and the move to an R8 luminance buffer; pipeline = downscale → separable H/V blur. — [Arm community](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/post-processing-effects-on-mobile-optimization-and-alternatives)
- **Jimenez, J. (2014). "Next Generation Post Processing in Call of Duty: Advanced Warfare." SIGGRAPH 2014 course *Advances in Real-Time Rendering in Games*.** Pyramidal filter hierarchy for bloom (temporal stability/robustness), explicit transparency handling for DoF/motion blur, Interleaved Gradient Noise. Slides OA on author site and course site. — [iryoku.com](https://www.iryoku.com/next-generation-post-processing-in-call-of-duty-advanced-warfare/); [Advances 2014 course page](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2014/index.html)
- **Hable, J. (2010). "Uncharted 2: HDR Lighting." GDC 2010, Sat 13 March, Room 305 South Hall (Naughty Dog).** Gamma/linear lighting, filmic tonemapping (the A–F + white-point curve), SSAO. Slides OA on SlideShare (naughty_dog). — [Naughty Dog GDC schedule](https://www.naughtydog.com/blog/gdc_sessions_saturday_march_13); [SlideShare](https://www.slideshare.net/naughty_dog/lighting-shading-by-john-hable)
- **Hable, J. "Filmic Tonemapping with Piecewise Power Curves" (filmicworlds.com blog, later follow-up).** Referenced by Unigine docs and tizian/tonemapper as the successor formulation. — [tizian/tonemapper](https://github.com/tizian/tonemapper); [Unigine docs](https://developer.unigine.com/en/docs/2.16/editor2/settings/render_settings/color/)
- **Narkowicz, K. (2016-01-06). "ACES Filmic Tone Mapping Curve." knarkowicz.wordpress.com.** The widely copied 5-coefficient ACES RRT+ODT fit used in countless WebGL/three.js shaders. — [tizian/tonemapper listing](https://github.com/tizian/tonemapper); [pixls.us thread linking the post](https://discuss.pixls.us/t/tone-mapping-operators-in-art-ctl-scripts/50573)
- **Reinhard, E., Stark, M., Shirley, P. & Ferwerda, J. (2002). "Photographic Tone Reproduction for Digital Images." ACM Trans. Graph. 21(3) (SIGGRAPH 2002).** Global/local photographic operator. DOI 10.1145/566654.566575 — **unverified**. — [secondary attribution](https://github.com/tizian/tonemapper)
- **ACES tone mapping technical details (ACES Central docs).** Authoritative description of the RRT/ODT tone scale that the Narkowicz fit approximates. — [docs.acescentral.com](https://docs.acescentral.com/system-components/output-transforms/technical-details/tone-mapping/)

- **"Neural Bloom" (arXiv 2509.05963, 2025).** States that Dual Filter Kawase and high-quality Gaussian are now standard in post pipelines (context citation). OA. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05963v1)

### Inferences
- If the kaginight engine uses additive point sprites with soft falloff rather than a real bloom pass, the honest framing is "bloom-like glow via additive blending and a tone curve"; cite Hable/Narkowicz for the curve and Kawase/Bjørge as the mobile-bloom reference the design *avoids* for bandwidth reasons (verify against the actual shader before claiming).

### Gaps
- Original Kawase GDC 2003 slides and Bjørge SIGGRAPH 2015 notes PDF not directly retrieved; their content is reconstructed from secondary citations.

## Key Question 4: Web Audio API — spec, scheduling accuracy, latency, autoplay/user-gesture policy, WAC papers

### Takeaway
The Web Audio API became a W3C Recommendation on 17 June 2021; the standard scheduling pattern for a procedural loop is Chris Wilson's "A Tale of Two Clocks" (JS timer look-ahead + `AudioContext.currentTime`). Browser autoplay policy (Safari 11 / macOS High Sierra 2017; Chrome 66 for media in 2018 and Chrome 71 for Web Audio) *requires* the user-gesture gate the site implements with its SOUND button. Peer-reviewed mobile browser latency studies are thin; the best is a WAC 2025 round-trip-latency paper.

### Cited Findings
- **W3C (2021-06-17). "Web Audio API." W3C Recommendation REC-webaudio-20210617.** "JavaScript API for creating, shaping, and manipulating sounds directly in a Web browser"; editors listed from Mozilla and Google, former editors Raymond Toy, Chris Wilson, Chris Rogers; Proposed Rec 2021-05-06. OA. — [W3C REC](https://www.w3.org/TR/2021/REC-webaudio-20210617/); [W3C news](https://www.w3.org/news/2021/web-audio-api-is-a-w3c-recommendation/); [press release](https://www.w3.org/press-releases/2021/webaudio)
- **W3C Audio Working Group charter (2022).** Hongchan Choi (Google) and Matt Paradis listed as chairs; API maintained by refinement plus new features. — [charter draft](https://w3c.github.io/charter-drafts/2022/audio-2022.html)
- **Wilson, C. (2013). "A Tale of Two Clocks – Scheduling Web Audio with Precision." HTML5Rocks / web.dev.** `AudioContext.currentTime` exposes the audio subsystem's hardware clock; JS timers are ~ms precision and drift, so run a `setTimeout` scheduler that looks ahead and schedules events on the audio clock. OA. — [web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/audio-scheduling)
- **WAAClock (sebpiq).** Library implementing the look-ahead pattern ("executes your callback slightly before the given deadline"). — [GitHub](https://github.com/sebpiq/WAAClock)
- **Chrome: "Autoplay policy in Chrome" (developer.chrome.com, 2018).** Muted autoplay always allowed; autoplay with sound requires a prior user gesture on the domain or (desktop) a Media Engagement Index threshold; policy shipped April/May 2018 (Chrome 66). OA. — [developer.chrome.com/blog/autoplay](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/autoplay); [chromium.org autoplay page](https://www.chromium.org/audio-video/autoplay)
- **Chrome: "Web Audio, Autoplay Policy and Games" (developer.chrome.com).** Web Audio enforcement delayed from Chrome 66 to Chrome 71 after developer feedback; `AudioContext` created before a gesture starts suspended and must be `resume()`d. OA. — [developer.chrome.com/blog/web-audio-autoplay](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/web-audio-autoplay)
- **WebKit (2017). "Auto-Play Policy Changes for macOS." webkit.org blog (Safari team guest post).** Safari 11 / macOS High Sierra uses an inference engine to block media with sound from autoplaying on most sites; per-site user control; silent video blocked in background tabs; developers should assume `<video>`/`<audio>` need a user click and check whether autoplay was denied. OA. — [webkit.org](https://webkit.org/?p=7734)
- **WebKit Bugzilla 182506 (2018), filed by Hongchan Choi.** Request to implement AudioWorklet in WebKit; notes Chrome shipped AudioWorklet in M64 via Origin Trial. — [bugs.webkit.org](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=182506)
- **Choi, H. "Audio Worklet" and "Audio Worklet design pattern" (developer.chrome.com).** Official articles on the audio-rendering-thread model that replaced ScriptProcessorNode. — [developer.chrome.com/blog/audio-worklet](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/audio-worklet?hl=ru); [design pattern](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/audio-worklet-design-pattern/?hl=he)
- **Gil Panal, Richard & David (2025). "A Maximum Length Sequence-based method for robust round-trip latency estimation in online Digital Audio Workstations." Web Audio Conference 2025 (Best Poster Award). HAL hal-05154354.** MLS-based round-trip latency estimation evaluated across devices, browsers and OSes; code at github.com/gilpanal/weblatencytest. OA (HAL). — [HAL](https://hal.univ-lyon2.fr/S2A/hal-05154354v2); [Télécom Paris note](https://adasp.telecom-paris.fr/resources/2025-11-21-hiaudio-wac25-best-poster-award/)
- **Superpowered — "iOS and Android Audio Latency Test App" / "Web Browser Audio Latency Test."** Industry (non-peer-reviewed) measurement of lowest possible round-trip latency on iOS 8+/Android 3.0+, and a browser beep-to-mic test; describes ≤10 ms as professional quality. — [superpowered.com/latency](https://superpowered.com/latency); [browser test](https://superpowered.com/webbrowserlatency)
- **Kaufman, J. (2021). "Browser Audio Latency" (blog).** Desktop measurement: ~67 ms end-to-end in Chrome, ~55 ms in Firefox on a 2016 MacBook; lower with tuned settings; no mobile data. — [jefftk.com](https://jefftk.com/p/browser-audio-latency)
- **W3C public-audio list threads (2012–2014)** discussing I/O latency in Chrome/Firefox and noting the spec does not address it. — [2014 thread](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-audio/2014JulSep/0180.html); [2012 thread](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-audio/2012JanMar/0008.html)
- **Generative.fm (Alex Bainter).** Browser generative-music platform built on Tone.js / Web Audio — an example of the "ambient procedural loop in the browser" genre, not a paper. — [GitHub mirror](https://github.com/libreblog/generative.fm)
- **Research Catalogue entry on porting SuperCollider's server to the browser.** States that Web Audio plus WebAssembly make ambitious real-time generative sound pieces feasible in the browser. — [researchcatalogue.net](https://www.researchcatalogue.net/view/711664/1111185)

### Inferences
- The site's design (AudioContext created/resumed inside the SOUND tap; silent `<audio>` loop to defeat the iOS mute switch; `touchend → ctx.resume()`) is a direct consequence of the 2017 WebKit and 2018 Chrome policies cited above, and can be presented as "policy-compliant by construction".
- For "synced to the animation", the citable mechanism is Wilson's two-clock scheduling; if the site instead calls `KN_AUDIO.kick()` from the rAF loop, be explicit that sync is frame-driven (visual clock → audio event) rather than audio-clock-driven, and cite Wilson as the known trade-off.

### Gaps
- No 2016–2019 WAC paper measuring Web Audio latency/jitter on iOS vs Android was found; the WAC 2025 paper is the only peer-reviewed browser-latency study located.
- No WAC paper specifically on generative/procedural music engines was retrieved in this pass (Roberts' *Gibber* papers exist at NIME/ICMC/WAC but were not returned by the search and are therefore not cited).
- Whether Paul Adenot (Mozilla) is a named editor of the 2021 REC could not be confirmed from the results (the REC lists "Mozilla" generically).

## Key Question 5: Perception / UX of ambient motion and autoplaying sound; accessibility (WCAG 2.x, prefers-reduced-motion)

### Takeaway
The normative basis is WCAG SC 1.4.2 Audio Control (A), 2.2.2 Pause/Stop/Hide (A), 2.3.3 Animation from Interactions (AAA) and Technique C39 using `prefers-reduced-motion` (Media Queries Level 5, shipped by WebKit in Safari 10.1, 2017). Eye-tracking literature on animated web elements is mixed: motion captures attention during browsing but can reduce attention/memory and disrupt tasks; accessibility bodies consistently say sound must be user-initiated.

### Cited Findings
- **W3C WAI. "Understanding SC 2.2.2: Pause, Stop, Hide (Level A)."** Intent: avoid distracting users; "starts automatically" means motion not caused by a deliberate user action; animation started by indirect interaction (hover/scroll) may also fail 2.3.3; a pause control that only works while focused is insufficient; audio is deferred to 1.4.2. OA. — [WCAG 2.1 Understanding](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/pause-stop-hide); [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide)
- **WCAG SC 1.4.2 Audio Control (Level A)** — if audio plays automatically for more than 3 seconds, a pause/stop or independent volume control is required (wording via secondary sources; confirm on W3C page). — [Yale accessibility article](https://usability.yale.edu/digital-accessibility/accessibility-resources/accessibility-articles/animated-content-and-timing); [theadminbar summary](https://theadminbar.com/accessibility-weekly/pause-stop-hide/)
- **WCAG SC 2.3.3 Animation from Interactions (Level AAA)** — provide a way to disable interaction-triggered animation unless essential; reduced-motion handling must not block content. — [Deque University](https://dequeuniversity.com/resources/wcag2.1/2.3.3-animations-from-interactions)
- **W3C WAI Technique C39: "Using the CSS prefers-reduced-motion query to prevent motion."** OA. — [W3C C39](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/css/C39.html)
- **WebKit (2017). "Responsive Design for Motion."** WebKit supports `prefers-reduced-motion`, "part of CSS Media Queries Level 5, User Preferences", usable via CSS or `window.matchMedia()`; feature first proposed to CSSWG in 2014 alongside the iOS API; shipped in Safari 10.1 (macOS 10.12.4 / iOS 10.3, March 2017). — [webkit.org](https://webkit.org/blog/7551/responsive-design-for-motion/); [Kavanagh article on ship dates](https://johnkavanagh.co.uk/articles/respecting-reduced-motion-preferences-in-css/)
- **CSSWG (April 2017) PR to create Media Queries Level 5 and add `prefers-reduced-motion`**; 2020 issue proposing the feature mean "minimize non-essential motion" rather than all animation (adoption not confirmed). — [2017 archive](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-css-archive/2017Apr/0498.html); [2020 archive](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-css-archive/2020Oct/0212.html)
- **The A11y Project. "Don't auto-play video, music and more."** Screen-reader users navigate by listening, so sound on load "will interfere immensely"; media over a few seconds needs a stop/pause control. — [a11yproject.com](https://www.a11yproject.com/posts/never-use-auto-play/)
- **Hong Kong OGCIO Web Accessibility Handbook §6.5 "Difficulties in browsing websites with background audio."** Auto-playing sound is annoying, especially for screen-reader users; background sound should be user-initiated or easily switched off. — [OGCIO](https://www.ogcio.gov.hk/en/community/web_mobileapp_accessibility/promulgating_resources/handbook/top_ten_concerns/6_5_difficulties_in_browsing.htm)
- **axe rule `no-autoplay-audio`** (Deque axe 4.7): `<video>`/`<audio>` must not autoplay with sound beyond 3 s without controls. — [rocketvalidator mirror](https://rocketvalidator.com/accessibility-validation/axe/4.7/no-autoplay-audio.md)
- **Lee, J. & Ahn, J.-H. (2012). "Attention to Banner Ads and Their Effectiveness: An Eye-Tracking Approach." International Journal of Electronic Commerce 17(1).** Animated banners attracted *less* attention than static ones and weakened the attention→memory link; attitude toward the brand is influenced even without conscious notice. — [KAIST DSpace](https://dspace.kaist.ac.kr/handle/10203/101840); [IJEC](https://www.ijec-web.org/?p=1783)
- **Hong, W., Cheung, M. & Thong, J.Y.L. (2021). Journal of the AIS 22(1).** Isolated motion and looming as features that capture attention during *browsing*; no animation feature had a significant effect during *searching* tasks. — [AIS eLibrary](https://aisel.aisnet.org/jais/vol22/iss1/2)
- **Simola et al. (Finland) — eye-tracking on real web pages.** Ads were overtly attended during reading; dwell times on ads longest when one ad was static and the other animated; human vision is sensitive to motion. — [University of Turku record](https://research.utu.fi/converis/portal/detail/Publication/3550924?lang=en_GB)
- **ToCHI 2004 paper on animated banners (hosted at thoughtcrumbs.com).** Animated banner ads interfere with common Web tasks yet are no more memorable than static ads (author attribution not verified in this pass). — [PDF](https://thoughtcrumbs.com/publications/ToCHI04.pdf)
- **Frontiers in Psychology (2017) 8:881.** Animation in banner advertising is a salient feature that captures visual attention; a featured face's gaze direction can guide attention further. OA. — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00881/full)
- **PMC5454066 (2017).** Related OA eye-tracking study on animated ads (details not extracted). — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5454066)
- **Practitioner evidence (non-academic):** IZEA quotes a consultant that background music "can make your audience jump ship immediately"; vendor blogs claim NN/g rated autoplay video among the most hated ad techniques and that unexpected motion/sound creates negative first impressions — NN/g primary pages were **not** retrieved, so treat as unverified. — [IZEA](https://izea.com/resources/4-website-features-that-drive-customers-nuts/); [reelflow (secondary NN/g claim)](https://www.reelflow.com/insights/should-videos-autoplay-on-our-website)

### Inferences
- A SOUND-button-only design satisfies WCAG 1.4.2 trivially (nothing plays automatically) and aligns with every accessibility body cited; the remaining obligations are 2.2.2 (a way to pause the always-moving jellyfish, or respecting `prefers-reduced-motion` via Technique C39) and 2.3.3 for tap-triggered reactions.
- The eye-tracking literature supports describing ambient motion as "attention-capturing during browsing but task-neutral during search" — a reasonable framing for a landing page whose job is a first impression.

### Gaps
- No Nielsen Norman Group primary article on autoplay sound was retrieved (only secondhand mentions).
- No controlled study measuring perceived quality or dwell time for *sound-on buttons* specifically was found; evidence is guideline-based.
- Official W3C Understanding pages for 1.4.2 and 2.3.3 were not fetched directly; wording is from secondary summaries.

## Key Question 6: Point-cloud / volumetric capture of performers (to link the aesthetic to volumetric performance)

### Takeaway
The academic anchors are Collet et al. 2015 (Microsoft, first end-to-end streamable free-viewpoint video), Orts-Escolano et al. 2016 *Holoportation* (UIST), and Schreer et al. 2019 (Fraunhofer HHI, 16 stereo pairs fused into a single point cloud). Intel True View and Canon's volumetric system surfaced only as product/patent/press mentions, not technical papers.

### Cited Findings
- **Collet, A., Chuang, M., Sweeney, P., Gillett, D., Evseev, D., Calabrese, D., Hoppe, H., Kirk, A. & Sullivan, S. (2015). "High-quality streamable free-viewpoint video." ACM Trans. Graph. 34(4) (SIGGRAPH 2015). DOI 10.1145/2766945.** Dense RGB+IR capture of performances → dynamic textured surfaces → MPEG stream; "first end-to-end solution" for FVV in a compact stream. OA on Hoppe's site. — [hhoppe.com project](https://hhoppe.com/proj/fvv/); [PDF](https://hhoppe.com/fvv.pdf); [researchr bib](https://researchr.org/publication/ColletCSGECHKS15/bibtex)
- **Orts-Escolano, S., Rhemann, C., Fanello, S., Izadi, S. et al. (2016). "Holoportation: Virtual 3D Teleportation in Real-time." Proc. UIST 2016, pp. 741–754. DOI 10.1145/2984511.2984517.** Real-time multi-camera 3D capture and transmission of people into AR/VR. — [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=509519); [UA record](https://observatorio-cientifico.ua.es/documentos/63d5b408f851ee1ba3e9f031); [citation page](https://ruofeidu.com/cites/Orts-Escolano2016Holoportation.html)
- **Schreer, O., Feldmann, I., Renault, S., Zepp, M., Eisert, P. & Kauff, P. (2019). "Capture and 3D Video Processing of Volumetric Video." Proc. IEEE ICIP 2019, Taipei.** 3DHBR system: 16 stereo pairs of high-resolution cameras capturing a moving person in 360°; stereo depth from all perspectives fused into one consistent 3D point cloud; studio commercialised as VoluCap GmbH. OA via Fraunhofer Publica. — [Fraunhofer Publica](https://publica.fraunhofer.de/handle/publica/410505); [HHI workflow page](https://www.hhi.fraunhofer.de/en/vit-imc/research-topics/volumetric-video-workflow.html)
- **Schreer et al. "Advanced Volumetric Capture and Processing." SMPTE Motion Imaging Journal 128(5).** Journal companion to the ICIP paper. — [SMPTE](https://journal.smpte.org/periodicals/SMPTE%20Motion%20Imaging%20Journal/128/5/13/)
- **Schreer, O. (2023). Keynote slides "Volumetric Video", CWI Spring School.** OA overview deck. — [CWI PDF](https://www.dis.cwi.nl/files/spring-school/Oliver_Schreer_2023_03_KeynoteSpringSchoolCWI_VolumetricVideo_public.pdf)
- **"FVV Live: A real-time free-viewpoint video system with consumer electronics hardware" (arXiv 2007.00558, 2020).** OA follow-on using consumer hardware. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.00558)
- **Intel True View** — only an Intel patent on colouring points in 3D video (real + virtual camera selection) and a Yale CCAM mention of Intel Studios' volumetric capture for *Grease* (2019) were found; no technical paper. — [Yale CCAM](https://ccam.yale.edu/news/exploring-volumetric-video-capture-early-steps)
- **Canon Volumetric Video System** — nothing retrievable in this pass (**gap**).

### Inferences
- The volumetric literature is overwhelmingly *mesh* (Collet, Holoportation) or *point-cloud-then-mesh* (Schreer); the kaginight site renders a *procedural* point cloud of a jellyfish rather than captured performer data. The honest link is aesthetic/semantic ("the point-cloud look of volumetric performance capture, applied to a procedural creature"), which also respects the owner's rule of not using human point clouds.

### Gaps
- Canon and Intel True View technical documentation not found; MPEG V-PCC / G-PCC standards (ISO/IEC 23090-5/9) were not searched within budget and would be the right standards citation for point-cloud streaming.
