# Hand/Body Tracking for Air-Drumming, Gesture-to-Sound Mapping, and Embodied Musical Interaction (2010–2026, with foundational works back to 2001)

Scope note for the report writer: this is a citation list compiled for XR drummer Kyoichi Kagiyama (Aerodrums-style air drums → Apple Vision Pro / iPhone, stick motion → OSC/MIDI → visuals; GitHub kagi920). Every entry gives authors, year, venue, DOI/URL, 1–2 sentence summary, open-access (OA) status, and measured numbers where the source states them. Entries whose bibliographic details I could confirm only through search snippets (not full text) are marked **[snippet-verified]**; entries from prior knowledge that I could not re-check in this session are marked **[unverified]**. Research-tool limitations: web fetches of full texts were blocked by the sandbox proxy (DNS/403) and the shared search budget ran out before the final round, so several numeric claims come from abstracts and secondary summaries rather than full papers.

## KQ1. Canonical papers on virtual/air drumming systems and drum-stroke detection (camera, IMU, controller-based)

### Takeaway
There is a 20-year NIME/HCI lineage of "virtual drum" and "air percussion" systems (magnetic tracker → VR controllers → depth cameras → webcam deep learning → IMU + headset fusion), and the recurring finding is that physical sticks and stable tracking improve timing accuracy, while markerless camera or IMU-only stroke detection must solve onset latency explicitly (often by predicting the hit from preparatory motion). Aerodrums itself (high-speed camera + retro-reflective stick markers) is commercial and has no peer-reviewed evaluation that I could find.

### Cited Findings
- **Havel, C., & Desainte-Catherine, M. (2004). "Modeling an Air Percussion for Composition and Performance." NIME 2004.** Virtual percussion in which strike recognition is built on a Flock of Birds magnetic tracker rather than a camera; a percussionist plays on a virtual percussion. OA (NIME archive). — [NIME archive](https://nime.org/proc/nime2004_havel/) **[snippet-verified]**
- **Mäki-Patola, T. (2005). "User Interface Comparison for Virtual Drums." NIME 2005, pp. 144–147, Vancouver. DOI 10.5281/zenodo.1176784.** 16 participants played a virtual drum in five interface setups, matching hits to metronome clicks; a physical stick was more accurate than a virtual computer-graphic stick, visual feedback of the drum slightly increased accuracy over audio-only, and alternating two-sided hits "through" the drum plate were less accurate than conventional one-sided strokes; most subjects preferred the physical stick. OA (NIME archive). Numbers (effect sizes) not visible in abstract. — [NIME archive](https://nime.org/proc/makipatola2005b) **[snippet-verified]**
- **Mäki-Patola, T., Laitinen, J., Kanerva, A., & Takala, T. (2005). "Experiments with Virtual Reality Instruments." NIME 2005.** Companion paper describing VR instrument prototypes (including the virtual drum) with tracked physical props; OA (NIME archive). — [NIME archive](https://nime.org/proc/nime2005_makipatola/) **[snippet-verified; co-author list unverified]**
- **Collicutt, M., Casciato, C., & Wanderley, M. M. (2009). "From Real to Virtual: A Comparison of Input Devices for Percussion Tasks." NIME 2009.** Compared a tom drum, a V-Drum pad, the Buchla Lightning and the Radio Baton on percussion tasks; subjects' timing variability was significantly worse on the Lightning and Radio Baton than on the familiar tom and V-Drum. OA (NIME archive). — [NIME archive](https://nime.org/proc/nime2009_collicutt/) **[snippet-verified; co-authors unverified]**
- **Tindale, A. R., Kapur, A., Tzanetakis, G., Driessen, P., & Schloss, A. (2005). "A Comparison of Sensor Strategies for Capturing Percussive Gestures." NIME 2005, pp. 200–203. DOI 10.5281/zenodo.1176828.** Surveys sensing techniques used by research and commercial drum controllers and argues that obtaining both quick response time and low-level expressive data (e.g., position) is difficult; frames the sensor trade-off space for any air-drum design. OA. — [NIME archive](https://nime.org/proc/tindale2005) **[snippet-verified]**
- **Tindale, A. R., et al. (2007). "A Hybrid Method for Extended Percussive Gesture." NIME 2007.** Uses timbre recognition to identify striking implement and location as symbolic data controlling a physical model (indirect acquisition of drum gesture from audio). OA. — [NIME archive](https://nime.org/proc/nime2007_tindale/) **[snippet-verified]**
- **Tavares, T. F., & Bertoloto, L. (2023). "Air Drums, and Bass: Anticipating Musical Gestures in Accelerometer Signals with a Lightweight CNN." IEEE 33rd Int. Workshop on Machine Learning for Signal Processing (MLSP 2023). DOI 10.1109/MLSP55844.2023.10285920.** A small CNN predicts drum hits/note onsets from accelerometer data, explicitly to beat the latency of threshold/peak-picking detection; the authors report the network can anticipate hits from preparatory motion (stick lift). Not OA on IEEE; author copy in Insper repository. Quantitative latency/accuracy figures not retrieved. — [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10285920); [Insper repository](https://repositorio.insper.edu.br/handle/11224/6703) **[snippet-verified]**
- **"A2D: Anywhere Anytime Drumming" (2023). arXiv:2304.03289.** Fine-tunes YOLOv5 on a custom dataset of air/surface drumming to detect generic, marker-less drumsticks from a plain webcam or smartphone camera. OA (arXiv). Authors, venue and FPS/latency numbers not retrieved (fetch blocked). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03289) **[snippet-verified; authors unverified]**
- **Banquiero, M., Valdeolivas, G., & Juan, M.-C. (2025). "Enhancing Musical Learning Through Mixed Reality: A Case Study Using PocketDrum and Meta Quest 3 for Drum Practice." Sensors 25(22), 6836. DOI 10.3390/s25226836.** Fuses an inertial drumstick device (PocketDrum) with Quest 3 hand tracking and color passthrough; the fused system corrected 19.1% of the drum-assignment errors made by the inertial-only setup, and the authors measured latency, jitter and sampling rate across components (figures not retrieved). OA (MDPI/PMC). — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/25/22/6836); [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12656102/) **[snippet-verified]**
- **Boem, A., & Turchet, L. (2024). "Selection as Tapping: An evaluation of 3D input techniques for timing tasks in musical Virtual Reality." International Journal of Human-Computer Studies 185, 103231.** 30 participants (with/without musical training) tapped with five VR selection techniques, one- and two-handed; techniques produced distinct asynchrony and variance, and "tracking stability emerged as a critical factor." Author manuscript in Trento repository (OA green); publisher version Elsevier. Per-technique ms values not retrieved. — [Trento IRIS record](https://iris.unitn.it/handle/11572/429251); [author PDF](https://iris.unitn.it/retrieve/49dcd12c-b95d-4954-9f43-0207e8b37aa3/1-s2.0-S1071581924000156-main.pdf) **[snippet-verified]**
- **Aerodrums (commercial, no peer review).** Uses a high-speed camera and retro-reflective markers on the sticks; the camera tracks marker motion to decide when to trigger sounds. — [New Atlas (Aerodrums 2)](https://newatlas.com/music/aerodrums-2-system/?amp=true); [Ahram](https://english.ahram.org.eg/News/123832.aspx)
- **Naidu & Fernandez (2012), MIT 6.111 student project**: colored-glove webcam air drum; not peer-reviewed, listed only as prior art. — [MIT report](https://fpga.mit.edu/videos/2012/team08/report.pdf)
- **Martin, C., & Lai, C.-H. (2011). "Strike on Stage: a percussion and media performance." NIME 2011.** Computer-vision system follows two percussionists while a rear projection responds to their playing; early example of vision-tracked drumming driving live visuals. OA (NIME, arXiv 2012.00250). — [NIME archive](https://nime.org/proc/nime2011_martin/); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.00250) **[snippet-verified]**

### Inferences
- The Mäki-Patola (2005) and Collicutt (2009) results together imply that an air-drum design should keep a physical stick (even if untethered) and should expect higher timing variance on unfamiliar gestural controllers — directly relevant to justifying Aerodrums-style sticks over bare-hand tracking.
- The 2023–2025 papers (Tavares & Bertoloto; Banquiero et al.) show the current research frontier is exactly Kagiyama's architecture: IMU or camera stroke detection fused with headset hand tracking, with explicit latency measurement and onset prediction.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed evaluation of Aerodrums was found.
- Quantitative FPS/latency figures for A2D and the Tavares CNN, and the per-technique asynchrony table in Boem & Turchet, were not retrievable (fetch blocked).
- I did not find a dedicated paper on drum-stroke onset detection from headset hand-tracking (Vision Pro/Quest) alone; the closest is Banquiero et al. 2025.

## KQ2. Action–sound latency and perceptual thresholds for percussion

### Takeaway
For percussive DMIs the empirical consensus is: ~10 ms stable latency is indistinguishable from zero, 20 ms is noticeably worse, and ±3 ms jitter at 10 ms is rated as badly as 20 ms (Jack et al. 2018); the "10 ms / 1 ms jitter" target comes from Wessel & Wright (2002). Drummers under delayed auditory feedback start compensating around ~55 ms (Dahl & Bresin 2001), and for continuous gesture instruments without tactile feedback the JND is ~20–30 ms (Mäki-Patola & Hämäläinen 2004).

### Cited Findings
- **Wessel, D., & Wright, M. (2002). "Problems and Prospects for Intimate Musical Control of Computers." Computer Music Journal 26(3), 11–22 (earlier NIME 2001).** Argues computer instruments need "very low, consistent latency"; widely cited for the recommendation of <10 ms latency with <1 ms jitter, and introduces OSC. OA via NIME archive/arXiv 2010.01570. — [NIME archive](https://nime.org/proc/nime2001_wessel/); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.01570) **[snippet-verified; CMJ volume/pages unverified]**
- **Jack, R. H., Mehrabi, A., Stockman, T., & McPherson, A. (2018). "Action-sound Latency and the Perceived Quality of Digital Musical Instruments: Comparing Professional Percussionists and Amateur Musicians." Music Perception 36(1), 109–128. DOI 10.1525/mp.2018.36.1.109.** Both professional percussionists and amateurs preferred 0 ms over 10 ms ± 3 ms jitter and over 20 ms; stable 10 ms did not differ significantly from 0 ms, so a stable 10 ms is acceptable where 20 ms is not; percussionists were more aware of latency and showed less timing variation under latency, an advantage that shrank as timing demands rose; the authors state the true threshold lies somewhere between 10 and 20 ms. Green OA (QMRO accepted manuscript). — [QMRO PDF](https://qmro.qmul.ac.uk/xmlui/bitstream/handle/123456789/44614/Jack%20Action-sound%20Latency%202018%20Accepted.pdf?sequence=1); [Sonic Field summary](https://labs.sonicfield.org/library/action-sound-latency-and-the-perceived-quality-of-digital-musical-instruments) **[journal volume/DOI unverified in this session]**
- **Jack, R. H., Stockman, T., & McPherson, A. (2016). "Effect of latency on performer interaction and subjective quality assessment of a digital musical instrument." Audio Mostly 2016, pp. 116–123. DOI 10.1145/2986416.2986428.** Musicians played a percussive DMI under 0, 10, 20 and 10 ± 3 ms jitter conditions; the paper studies both quality ratings and changes in performer behaviour. OA author PDF. — [instrumentslab PDF](https://instrumentslab.org/data/andrew/jack_am2016.pdf); [DOI](https://www.doi.org/10.1145/2986416.2986428) **[snippet-verified]**
- **McPherson, A., Jack, R. H., & Moro, G. (2016). "Action-Sound Latency: Are Our Tools Fast Enough?" NIME 2016.** Benchmarks the round-trip latency of common DMI toolchains (e.g., Arduino/Raspberry Pi/laptop stacks) against the 10 ms target and finds many exceed it; motivates measuring one's own OSC/MIDI chain. OA. — [NIME archive](https://nime.org/proc/nime2016_mcpherson/) **[snippet-verified; content from prior knowledge]**
- **Dahl, S., & Bresin, R. (2001). "Is the Player More Influenced by the Auditory Than the Tactile Feedback from the Instrument?" DAFx-01.** Drummers played with a metronome while auditory feedback was delayed; players initiated strokes earlier as delay increased, timing errors suggested a possible breakpoint around 55 ms, beyond which keeping a steady rhythm became harder. OA (DAFx archive). — [DAFx archive](https://dafx.de/paper-archive/details/LKHefqWVPCGLn8Sy0EG00A) **[snippet-verified]**
- **Mäki-Patola, T., & Hämäläinen, P. (2004). "Latency Tolerance for Gesture Controlled Continuous Sound Instrument without Tactile Feedback." ICMC 2004.** Theremin-style continuous-control study; just-noticeable latency difference about 20–30 ms. OA author PDF. — [Aalto PDF](https://users.aalto.fi/~hamalap5/publications/icmcarticlefinal10.pdf) **[snippet-verified]**
- **Lester, M., & Boley, J. (2007). "The Effects of Latency on Live Sound Monitoring." AES Convention 123, paper 7198.** Subjective test of objectionable monitoring latency across instruments and wedge vs in-ear monitoring; acceptable latency ranged from ~42 ms down to possibly under 1.4 ms depending on instrument and setup, with in-ears less tolerant. Not OA (AES members). — [AES E-Library](https://aes2.org/publications/elibrary-page/?id=14256) **[snippet-verified]**
- **Larsen, J. V., & Knoche, H. (2017). (Delayed auditory feedback on strumming.) NIME 2017.** 250 ms delay hardly affected musically trained participants but degraded untrained participants — shows expertise moderates DAF effects (non-percussion). OA. — [NIME 2017 PDF](https://vbn.aau.dk/ws/files/295153538/nime2017_paper0086.pdf) **[snippet-verified; title unverified]**
- **Audio-latency JND work (Regensburg, 2024, ACM).** Recent JND-of-audio-latency studies exist (ACM DOI 10.1145/3678299.3678331); details not retrieved. — [Regensburg ePub](https://epub.uni-regensburg.de/59003/3/3678299.3678331.pdf) **[unverified content]**

### Inferences
- For a Vision Pro pipeline whose hand-tracking latency alone is on the order of 100+ ms (see KQ3), the Jack et al. thresholds imply that sound must be triggered by a faster path (stick IMU or predictive onset, cf. Tavares & Bertoloto 2023) while the headset tracking is reserved for visuals/position.
- Jitter matters as much as mean latency (10 ± 3 ms ≈ 20 ms in ratings), so OSC over WiFi should be timestamped/smoothed.

### Gaps
- No study specifically measures latency tolerance for *air* drumming (no rebound) versus pad drumming; the percussion thresholds above come from a pad-type DMI.

## KQ3. Hand-tracking accuracy and latency: Apple Vision Pro, Meta Quest, Leap Motion/Ultraleap, Azure Kinect, MediaPipe

### Takeaway
Peer-reviewed, marker-referenced benchmarks exist for Quest 2 (≈1.1 cm fingertip error, ≈9.6° joint error, ≈45 ms delay), Leap Motion (sub-mm static, ~1.2 mm dynamic; LMC2 palm error 5.2–5.3 mm vs 7.9–9.8 mm LMC1), Azure Kinect (RMSE 20–89 mm vs mocap; depth error <11 mm) and MediaPipe vs LMC vs Vicon (joint-angle RMSE ~11–22°). For Apple Vision Pro there is **no peer-reviewed accuracy benchmark** found; the only latency number is a non-peer-reviewed frame-counting estimate of ~127.7 ms (vs ~70 ms Quest 3), plus one Sensors 2025 application paper reporting 60 FPS operation.

### Cited Findings
- **Abdlkarim, D., Di Luca, M., Aves, P., Maeda, M., Yeo, S.-H., Miall, R. C., Wing, A. M., & Galea, J. M. (2023/2024). "A methodological framework to assess the accuracy of virtual reality hand-tracking systems: A case study with the Meta Quest 2." Behavior Research Methods 56(2), 1052–1063. DOI 10.3758/s13428-022-02051-8.** Marker-based ground truth and a fixed target grid; average fingertip position error ≈1.1 cm, joint-angle error ≈9.6°, temporal delay ≈45.0 ms; proposes lens-distortion correction. OA (PMC). — [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10830632/); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13428-022-02051-8) **[snippet-verified; full author list from prior knowledge]**
- **Weichert, F., Bachmann, D., Rudak, B., & Fisseler, D. (2013). "Analysis of the Accuracy and Robustness of the Leap Motion Controller." Sensors 13(5), 6380–6393. DOI 10.3390/s130506380.** Industrial-robot reference pen: deviation <0.2 mm static, ~1.2 mm dynamic. OA. — [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3690061/) **[snippet-verified]**

OA. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10214v1) **[snippet-verified]**

- **Azure Kinect vs Vicon (26 subjects, lateral reach).** Angular MAE ≈5–15° for upper joints, higher for lower body. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9860777) **[authors unverified]**
- **Apple Vision Pro — Sensors 2025 (PMC12390506): "Real-Time Hand Tracking and Collision Detection for Immersive Mixed-Reality Boxing Training on Apple Vision Pro."** 12 participants; gesture-recognition accuracy 96.3%, technique-validation accuracy 88.5%; system "consistently operated at 60 FPS with low latency." Application-level only, no joint-level error. OA. — [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12390506/) **[snippet-verified; authors unverified]**
- **Apple Vision Pro latency (non-peer-reviewed, Road to VR 2024).** Frame-counting of passthrough hand vs rendered hand: ≈3.5 frames at 30 FPS capture (116.7 ms) + ≈11 ms passthrough ≈ **127.7 ms** photon-to-hand; no difference between skeletal tracking and tap detection, no lighting effect; Quest 3 estimated ≈70 ms; visionOS 2 added hand-tracking *prediction* that reduces perceived (not true) latency. — [Road to VR](https://roadtovr.com/apple-vision-pro-meta-quest-3-hand-tracking-latency-comparison/); [visionOS 2 note](https://roadtovr.com/?p=172257)
- **Apple Vision Pro developer forum anecdote**: intermittent 5–10 s hand-tracking lag when a UITextView becomes active (CPU spike) — single anecdote. — [Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/800604)

### Inferences
- Combining Abdlkarim's Quest 2 delay (45 ms) with Jack et al.'s 10–20 ms percussion threshold shows headset hand tracking alone is 2–4× too slow for sound triggering; Vision Pro's ~128 ms (informal) is worse still. This is the strongest quantitative argument for Kagiyama's IMU/camera-stick → OSC path with headset tracking used for visuals and calibration.
- The two 2025 Sensors papers disagree on MediaPipe vs Leap ordering, so any claim about which is "more accurate" should be framed as setup-dependent.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed marker-referenced accuracy/latency benchmark for Apple Vision Pro hand tracking was found; I could not confirm visionOS 2's advertised 90 Hz ARKit hand-tracking rate or prediction API from a primary source (search budget exhausted).
- No Ultraleap Gemini end-to-end latency measurement retrieved.
- No study evaluating any of these trackers specifically on fast percussive (stick-speed) motion was found; the boxing paper (Vision Pro) and PocketDrum paper (Quest 3) are the closest.

## KQ4. Embodied music cognition and sensorimotor synchronization foundations (Leman, Repp, Godøy, Jensenius)

### Takeaway
The foundational set is: Leman (2008) on embodied music cognition and mediation technology; Godøy & Leman (eds., 2010) *Musical Gestures* with the Jensenius–Wanderley–Godøy–Leman gesture taxonomy chapter; Godøy (2003) motor-mimetic cognition; Godøy, Haga & Jensenius (2006) on "air instrument" mimicry (the most directly on-point for air drumming); Jensenius (2007) *Action–Sound* thesis; Repp (2005) and Repp & Su (2013) tapping/SMS reviews; Dahl (2004) and Dahl & Friberg (2007) on drumming movement and its visual expressiveness; Maes, Leman, Palmer & Wanderley (2014) on action-based effects on perception.

## KQ5. Haptics and the absence of physical rebound in air drumming

### Takeaway
Direct studies of "air drumming without rebound" are scarce; the relevant evidence is indirect: haptic cues dominate auditory cues in controlling striking velocity (Cao et al. 2016), a physical stick beats a virtual stick for timing (Mäki-Patola 2005), haptic guidance improves percussion learning (Grindlay 2007; Holland et al. 2010), and the NIME vibrotactile-feedback literature (Marshall & Wanderley 2006) frames how to add tactile cues to a DMI.

### Cited Findings
- **Cao, Y., et al. (2016). "The dominance of haptics over audition in controlling wrist velocity during striking movements." Experimental Brain Research.** Participants struck a virtual object of computer-controlled stiffness with haptic and auditory cues perturbed separately; striking velocity compensated more for haptic than auditory changes, attributed to lifelong somatosensory experience of objects. OA (PMC). — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4785215) **[snippet-verified; full author list unverified]**
- **Mäki-Patola, T. (2005). NIME 2005 (see KQ1).** Physical stick more accurate and preferred over virtual stick — the clearest empirical signal on tactile grounding in virtual drumming. — [NIME archive](https://nime.org/proc/makipatola2005b)
- **Dahl & Bresin (2001). DAFx (see KQ2).** Title question — whether auditory or tactile feedback dominates — with players shifting stroke onset under delayed audio. — [DAFx](https://dafx.de/paper-archive/details/LKHefqWVPCGLn8Sy0EG00A)
- **Grindlay, G. (2007). *Haptic Guidance Benefits Musical Motor Learning*. MIT M.S. thesis.** Haptic guidance added to audio cut final velocity-recall error by 17% and early timing error by 18% vs audio alone in a percussion task. OA. — [MIT thesis PDF](https://opera.media.mit.edu/articles/Grindlay_MSthesis_final.pdf) **[snippet-verified; thesis title unverified]**
- **Holland, S., Bouwer, A. J., Dalgleish, M., & Hurtig, T. M. (2010). "Feeling the beat where it counts: fostering multi-limb rhythm skills with the haptic drum kit." TEI 2010.** Four vibrotactile actuators (wrists/ankles) guide multi-limb drum patterns; beginners could learn intricate patterns from haptic stimuli alone, though haptic+audio was preferred; theory grounded in Dalcroze, entrainment and sensorimotor-contingency theory. OA (ORO). — [Open University ORO](https://oro.open.ac.uk/18900) **[snippet-verified]**
- **Bouwer, Holland et al. — Haptic iPod (2011) and Haptic Bracelets (2013 chapter).** Passive learning of multi-limb rhythms via wrist/ankle vibrotactiles while reading. — [ORO](https://oro.open.ac.uk/35072) **[snippet-verified]**
- **Marshall, M. T., & Wanderley, M. M. (2006). "Vibrotactile Feedback in Digital Musical Instruments." NIME 2006.** Surveys and prototypes vibrotactile feedback in DMIs; design reference for adding tactile cues to air-drum sticks. OA. — [NIME archive](https://nime.org/proc/nime2006_marshall/) **[snippet-verified]**
- **Bouënard, A., Wanderley, M. M., & Gibet, S. (2010). "Gesture Control of Sound Synthesis: Analysis and Classification of Percussion Gestures." Acta Acustica united with Acustica 96(4), 668–677. DOI 10.3813/AAA.918321.** Motion-captured timpani gestures analysed/classified, reduced-dimension representation drives a physics-based virtual percussionist whose dynamics (including stick–membrane contact/rebound) drive sound synthesis. Not OA (HAL preprint available). — [HAL](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-00763258); [IDMIL](https://idmil.org/?p=2827) **[snippet-verified]**
- **Bouënard, A., Wanderley, M. M., & Gibet, S. (2008). "Enhancing the Visualization of Percussion Gestures by Virtual Character Animation." NIME 2008.** OA. — [NIME archive](https://nime.org/proc/nime2008_bouenard/) **[snippet-verified]**
- **Meta Reality Labs — "Perceptual Limits of Visual-Haptic Simultaneity in Virtual Reality Interactions."** Relevant to timing of haptic pulses relative to visual hits in VR (details not retrieved). — [Meta Research](https://research.fb.com/publications/perceptual-limits-of-visual-haptic-simultaneity-in-virtual-reality-interactions/) **[unverified content]**
- Stanford ME327 "Haptic Drum-Hero" (2023) — course project with Hapkit sticks, no results; prior art only. — [Stanford](https://charm.stanford.edu/ME327/2023-Group2)

### Inferences
- Because haptics dominate velocity control (Cao 2016), air drumming must substitute another cue for rebound — auditory or vibrotactile in the stick — to keep dynamics consistent; this is a defensible research-grounded design argument.

### Gaps
- No controlled study measuring timing/velocity accuracy of air drumming *with vs without* stick rebound or vibrotactile substitution was found.
- Cao et al. full author list and Grindlay thesis title not confirmed.

## KQ6. Mapping strategies, DMI design frameworks (NIME), drum-driven generative visuals

### Takeaway
Mapping theory rests on Hunt & Wanderley (2002), Hunt, Wanderley & Paradis (2003) and Wanderley & Depalle (2004); DMI frameworks on Wanderley & Orio (2002), Malloch et al. (2006), Jordà (2004) and Wessel & Wright (2002). Percussion-driven audiovisual work has a NIME thread from Strike on Stage (2011) through PIGS (2016) to Cymbalism (2025), which argues visuals should be designed *first* and shape composition.

### Cited Findings
- **Hunt, A., & Wanderley, M. M. (2002). "Mapping performer parameters to synthesis engines." Organised Sound 7(2), 97–108. DOI 10.1017/S1355771802002030.** Defines mapping as the designed link between interface and sound source, reviews one-to-one vs many-to-many strategies, proposes a general model and guidelines. Not OA. — [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/organised-sound/article/mapping-performer-parameters-to-synthesis-engines/BD8AAF4BC582D3DCACB5AEC5BF49F8FE); [IDMIL](https://idmil.org/?p=2858) **[snippet-verified]**
- **Hunt, A., Wanderley, M. M., & Paradis, M. (2003). "The importance of parameter mapping in electronic instrument design." Journal of New Music Research 32(4), 429–440.** Experiments showing complex (many-to-many) mappings are more engaging and support better performance than simple one-to-one mappings. Not OA. **[unverified in this session; prior knowledge]**
- **Wanderley, M. M., & Depalle, P. (2004). "Gestural control of sound synthesis." Proceedings of the IEEE 92(4), 632–644.** Review of gesture acquisition, mapping and synthesis for DMIs. Not OA. — cited in [Sonic Field](https://labs.sonicfield.org/library/mapping-performer-parameters-to-synthesis-engines) **[snippet-verified existence; DOI unverified]**
- **Verfaille, V., Wanderley, M. M., & Depalle, P. (2006). "Mapping strategies for sound synthesis, digital audio effects, and sonification of performer gestures."** Extends mapping theory to effects and sonification. — [Sonic Field](https://labs.sonicfield.org/library/mapping-strategies-for-sound-synthesis-digital-audio-effects-and-sonification-of) **[snippet-verified; venue unverified]**
- **Malloch, J., Birnbaum, D., Sinyor, E., & Wanderley, M. M. (2006). "A New Conceptual Framework for Digital Musical Instruments." DAFx-06, Montreal, pp. 49–52.** Adapts a human-information-processing model to classify DMIs by performance context and performer behaviour, with a visualization for analysing/designing instruments. OA (DAFx archive). — [DAFx](https://www.dafx.de/paper-archive/details/3j-1bIzZgUCXzUo_UFCEeg); [IDMIL](https://www.idmil.org/?p=3123) **[snippet-verified]**

### Inferences
- Kagiyama's stick-motion → OSC → TouchDesigner architecture maps cleanly onto the Hunt & Wanderley layered-mapping model (gesture features → intermediate parameters → sound/visual parameters), and onto Wessel & Wright's OSC-based control model.
- Cymbalism (2025) is the most current peer-reviewed precedent for "drum-driven generative visuals as a compositional primary," useful for collaboration pitches.

### Gaps
- Several framework DOIs (Hunt et al. 2003; Wanderley & Depalle 2004; Wanderley & Orio 2002; Jordà 2004; Morreale & McPherson 2017) could not be re-verified because the search budget was exhausted; they are standard NIME citations but should be DOI-checked before use.
- No paper specifically on *air*-drum-driven generative visuals (as opposed to pad/MIDI drums) was found.
